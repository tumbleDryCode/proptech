The municipality has a total of 214,158 inhabitants<sup id=\"cite_ref-1\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-1\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>1<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> in an area of 97.40&#160;km<sup>2</sup>.<sup id=\"cite_ref-dgt_2-0\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-dgt-2\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>2<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> Cascais is an important <a href=\"/wiki/Tourism_in_Portugal\" title=\"Tourism in Portugal\">tourist destination</a>. Its <a href=\"/wiki/Cascais_Marina\" title=\"Cascais Marina\">marina</a> hosts events such as the <a href=\"/wiki/America%27s_Cup\" title=\"America&#39;s Cup\">America's Cup</a> and the town of <a href=\"/wiki/Estoril\" title=\"Estoril\">Estoril</a>, part of the Cascais municipality, hosts conferences such as the <a href=\"/wiki/Horasis_Global_Meeting\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Horasis Global Meeting\">Horasis Global Meeting</a>.\n</p><p>Cascais's history as a popular seaside resort originated in the 1870s, when King <a href=\"/wiki/Lu%C3%ADs_I_of_Portugal\" title=\"Lu\u00eds I of Portugal\">Lu\u00eds I of Portugal</a> and the <a href=\"/wiki/House_of_Braganza\" title=\"House of Braganza\">Portuguese royal family</a> made the seaside town their residence every September, thus also attracting members of the <a href=\"/wiki/Portuguese_nobility\" title=\"Portuguese nobility\">Portuguese nobility</a>, who established a summer community there. Cascais is known for the many members of royalty who have lived there, including King <a href=\"/wiki/Edward_VIII_of_the_United_Kingdom\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Edward VIII of the United Kingdom\">Edward VIII of the United Kingdom</a>, when he was the Duke of Windsor, King <a href=\"/wiki/Juan_Carlos_I_of_Spain\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Juan Carlos I of Spain\">Juan Carlos I of Spain</a>, and King <a href=\"/wiki/Umberto_II_of_Italy\" title=\"Umberto II of Italy\">Umberto II of Italy</a>. Exiled Cuban president <a href=\"/wiki/Fulgencio_Batista\" title=\"Fulgencio Batista\">Fulgencio Batista</a> was also once a resident of the municipality. The <a href=\"/wiki/Casino_Estoril\" title=\"Casino Estoril\">Casino Estoril</a> inspired <a href=\"/wiki/Ian_Fleming\" title=\"Ian Fleming\">Ian Fleming</a>'s first <a href=\"/wiki/James_Bond_(literary_character)\" title=\"James Bond (literary character)\">James Bond</a> novel, <a href=\"/wiki/Casino_Royale_(novel)\" title=\"Casino Royale (novel)\"><i>Casino Royale</i></a>.<sup id=\"cite_ref-3\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-3\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>3<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup>\n</p><p>The municipality is one of the wealthiest in both Portugal and the <a href=\"/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula\" title=\"Iberian Peninsula\">Iberian Peninsula</a>.<sup id=\"cite_ref-4\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-4\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>4<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id=\"cite_ref-5\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-5\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>5<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id=\"cite_ref-6\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-6\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>6<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id=\"cite_ref-7\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-7\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>7<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> It has one of the most expensive real estate markets and one of the highest costs of living in the country,<sup id=\"cite_ref-8\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-8\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>8<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id=\"cite_ref-9\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-9\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>9<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id=\"cite_ref-10\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-10\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>10<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id=\"cite_ref-11\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-11\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>11<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> and is consistently ranked highly for its quality of life.<sup id=\"cite_ref-12\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-12\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>12<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id=\"cite_ref-13\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-13\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>13<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup>\n</p>\n<meta property=\"mw:PageProp/toc\" />\n<div class=\"mw-heading mw-heading2\"><h2 id=\"History\">History</h2><span class=\"mw-editsection\"><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">[</span><a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Cascais&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1\" title=\"Edit section: History\"><span>edit</span></a><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">]</span></span></div>\n<figure class=\"mw-default-size mw-halign-left\" typeof=\"mw:File/Thumb\"><a href=\"/wiki/File:33037-Cascais_(35538412783).jpg\" class=\"mw-file-description\"><img src=\"//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2a/33037-Cascais_%2835538412783%29.jpg/220px-33037-Cascais_%2835538412783%29.jpg\" decoding=\"async\" width=\"220\" height=\"147\" class=\"mw-file-element\" srcset=\"//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2a/33037-Cascais_%2835538412783%29.jpg/330px-33037-Cascais_%2835538412783%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2a/33037-Cascais_%2835538412783%29.jpg/440px-33037-Cascais_%2835538412783%29.jpg 2x\" data-file-width=\"6016\" data-file-height=\"4016\" /></a><figcaption>Coast view of Cascais, Portugal.</figcaption></figure>\n<p>Human settlement of the territory today known as Cascais dates to the late <a href=\"/wiki/Paleolithic\" title=\"Paleolithic\">Paleolithic</a>, as indicated by remnants encountered in the north of Tala\u00edde, in Alto do Cabecinho (Tires) and south of Moinhos do Cabreiro.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_14-0\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-14\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>14<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> It was during the <a href=\"/wiki/Neolithic\" title=\"Neolithic\">Neolithic</a> that permanent settlements were established in the region, their inhabitants utilizing the natural grottoes (such as the <a href=\"/wiki/Caves_of_Po%C3%A7o_Velho\" title=\"Caves of Po\u00e7o Velho\">Caves of Po\u00e7o Velho</a> in Cascais) and artificial shelters (like those in <a href=\"/wiki/Artificial_caves_of_Alapraia\" title=\"Artificial caves of Alapraia\">Alapraia</a> or S\u00e3o Pedro) to deposit their dead. The bodies were buried along with offerings, a practice that continued to the <a href=\"/wiki/Chalcolithic\" title=\"Chalcolithic\">Chalcolithic</a>.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_14-1\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-14\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>14<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup>\n</p><p>Roman interventions in the area occurred with the settlement of the <i>villae</i> of <a href=\"/wiki/Roman_villa_of_Freiria\" title=\"Roman villa of Freiria\">Freiria</a> (today S\u00e3o Domingos de Rana) and <a href=\"/wiki/Roman_ruins_of_Casais_Velhos\" title=\"Roman ruins of Casais Velhos\">Casais Velhos</a> (Charneca), evidence for which includes a group of ten tanks discovered along the <i>Rua Marques Leal Pancada</i> in Cascais, which was the location of a salting factory for fish.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_14-2\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-14\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>14<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> Roman dominion over the territory also influenced place names in the region, as was the case with the word \"Caparide\" (from the Latin <i>capparis</i>, meaning \"<i><a href=\"/wiki/Caper\" title=\"Caper\">caper</a></i>\"), as well as several inscriptions associated with funerary graves.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_14-3\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-14\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>14<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup>\n</p><p>The <a href=\"/wiki/Visigoths\" title=\"Visigoths\">Visigoths</a> also left their mark especially on the Visigothic Cemetery of Alcoit\u00e3o,<sup id=\"cite_ref-15\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-15\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>15<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> as well as in the late-Roman and medieval necropolis of Tala\u00edde.<sup id=\"cite_ref-16\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-16\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>16<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id=\"cite_ref-17\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-17\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>17<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup>\n</p><p>Similarly, Muslim settlers in the region left their mark on local place names, including \"Alcoit\u00e3o\" and \"Alcabideche\", where the romantic poet Ibn Muqana al-Qabdaqi, who wrote of the region's agriculture and windmills, was born at the beginning of the 11th century.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_14-4\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-14\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>14<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> The discovery of several corpses in 1987 at Arneiro, in <a href=\"/wiki/Carcavelos\" title=\"Carcavelos\">Carcavelos</a>, led to the identification of fifteen burials that, due to their characteristics, made it possible to verify that the individuals buried there were of <a href=\"/wiki/Berbers\" title=\"Berbers\">Berber</a> origin.<sup id=\"cite_ref-18\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-18\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>18<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup>\n</p><p>The development of Cascais began in earnest in the 12th century, when it was administratively subordinate to the town of <a href=\"/wiki/Sintra\" title=\"Sintra\">Sintra</a>, located to the north. In its humble beginnings, Cascais depended on the products of the sea and land, but by the 13th century its fish production was also supplying the nearby city of <a href=\"/wiki/Lisbon\" title=\"Lisbon\">Lisbon</a>. The toponym \"<i>Cascais</i>\" appears to derive from this period, a plural derivation of <i>cascal</i> (<i>monte de cascas</i>) which signified a \"mountain of shells\", referring to the abundant volume of marine mollusks harvested from the coastal waters.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_14-5\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-14\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>14<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> During the 14th century, the population spread outside the walls of its <a href=\"/wiki/Citadel_of_Cascais\" title=\"Citadel of Cascais\">fortress castle</a>.\n</p>\n<figure class=\"mw-default-size mw-halign-left\" typeof=\"mw:File/Thumb\"><a href=\"/wiki/File:Walking_around_Cascais_(49663277256).jpg\" class=\"mw-file-description\"><img src=\"//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6b/Walking_around_Cascais_%2849663277256%29.jpg/220px-Walking_around_Cascais_%2849663277256%29.jpg\" decoding=\"async\" width=\"220\" height=\"165\" class=\"mw-file-element\" srcset=\"//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6b/Walking_around_Cascais_%2849663277256%29.jpg/330px-Walking_around_Cascais_%2849663277256%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6b/Walking_around_Cascais_%2849663277256%29.jpg/440px-Walking_around_Cascais_%2849663277256%29.jpg 2x\" data-file-width=\"4032\" data-file-height=\"3024\" /></a><figcaption>Center of Cascais (<a href=\"/wiki/Peter_I_of_Portugal\" title=\"Peter I of Portugal\">King Peter I</a> statue).</figcaption></figure>\n<p>The settlement's prosperity led to its administrative independence from Sintra in 1364. On 7 June 1364, the people of Cascais obtained from <a href=\"/wiki/Peter_I_of_Portugal\" title=\"Peter I of Portugal\">King Peter I</a> the elevation of the village to the status of town, necessitating the appointment of local judges and administrators. The townspeople were consequently obligated to pay the Crown 200 pounds of gold annually, as well as bearing the expense of paying the local administrators' salaries. Owing to the regions' wealth, these obligations were easily satisfied.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_14-6\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-14\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>14<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> The town and the surrounding lands were owned by a succession of feudal lords, the most famous of whom was <a href=\"/wiki/Jo%C3%A3o_das_Regras\" title=\"Jo\u00e3o das Regras\">Jo\u00e3o das Regras</a> (died 1404), a lawyer and professor of the <a href=\"/wiki/University_of_Coimbra\" title=\"University of Coimbra\">University of Lisbon</a> who was involved in the ascension of <a href=\"/wiki/John_I_of_Portugal\" title=\"John I of Portugal\">King John I</a> to power as the first King of the <a href=\"/wiki/House_of_Aviz\" title=\"House of Aviz\">House of Aviz</a>.\n</p><p>The castle of Cascais was likely constructed during this period, since by 1370, King <a href=\"/wiki/Ferdinand_I_of_Portugal\" title=\"Ferdinand I of Portugal\">Ferdinand</a> had donated the castle and Cascais to Gomes Louren\u00e7o de Avelar to hold as a seigneurial fiefdom.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_14-7\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-14\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>14<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> These privileges were then passed on to his successors, among them Jo\u00e3o das Regras and the Counts of Monsanto, and later the Marquess of Cascais.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_14-8\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-14\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>14<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> Meanwhile, despite its conquest and sack by <a href=\"/wiki/Crown_of_Castile\" title=\"Crown of Castile\">Castilian</a> forces in 1373, and blockade of the port in 1382 and 1384, Cascais continued to grow beyond its walls.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_14-9\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-14\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>14<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> By the end of the 14th century this resulted in the creation of the <a href=\"/wiki/Parish\" title=\"Parish\">parishes</a> of Santa Maria de Cascais, <a href=\"/wiki/Alcabideche\" title=\"Alcabideche\">S\u00e3o Vicente de Alcabideche</a> and <a href=\"/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Domingos_de_Rana\" title=\"S\u00e3o Domingos de Rana\">S\u00e3o Domingos de Rana</a>.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_14-10\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-14\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>14<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup>\n</p>\n<figure class=\"mw-default-size mw-halign-left\" typeof=\"mw:File/Thumb\"><a href=\"/wiki/File:Braun_Cascais_UBHD.jpg\" class=\"mw-file-description\"><img src=\"//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/64/Braun_Cascais_UBHD.jpg/220px-Braun_Cascais_UBHD.jpg\" decoding=\"async\" width=\"220\" height=\"61\" class=\"mw-file-element\" srcset=\"//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/64/Braun_Cascais_UBHD.jpg/330px-Braun_Cascais_UBHD.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/64/Braun_Cascais_UBHD.jpg/440px-Braun_Cascais_UBHD.jpg 2x\" data-file-width=\"2110\" data-file-height=\"586\" /></a><figcaption>A 1572 sketch of the coast of Cascais.</figcaption></figure>\n<p>From the <a href=\"/wiki/Middle_Ages\" title=\"Middle Ages\">Middle Ages</a> onward, Cascais depended on fishing, maritime commerce (it was a stop for ships sailing to Lisbon), and agriculture, producing <a href=\"/wiki/Wine\" title=\"Wine\">wine</a>, <a href=\"/wiki/Olive_oil\" title=\"Olive oil\">olive oil</a>, cereals, and fruits. Due to its location at the mouth of the <a href=\"/wiki/Tagus\" title=\"Tagus\">Tagus</a> estuary, it was also seen as a strategic post in the defence of Lisbon. Around 1488, <a href=\"/wiki/John_II_of_Portugal\" title=\"John II of Portugal\">King John II</a> built a small fortress in the town, situated by the sea. On 15 November 1514, <a href=\"/wiki/Manuel_I_of_Portugal\" title=\"Manuel I of Portugal\">Manuel I</a> conceded a <a href=\"/wiki/Foral\" title=\"Foral\">foral</a> (<i>charter</i>) to Cascais, instituting the region's municipal authority.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_14-11\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-14\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>14<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> It was followed on 11 June 1551 by a license from <a href=\"/wiki/John_III_of_Portugal\" title=\"John III of Portugal\">King John III</a> to institutionalise the <i>Santa Casa da Miseric\u00f3rdia de Cascais</i>.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_14-12\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-14\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>14<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> The <a href=\"/wiki/Mother_Church\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Mother Church\">Mother Church</a> of Cascais, the <a href=\"/wiki/Church_of_Nossa_Senhora_da_Assun%C3%A7%C3%A3o_(Cascais)\" title=\"Church of Nossa Senhora da Assun\u00e7\u00e3o (Cascais)\">Church of Nossa Senhora da Assun\u00e7\u00e3o</a>, dates back to the early 16th century. The town's medieval fortress was inadequate to repel invasions, and in 1580 Spanish troops led by the Duque of Alba took the village during the conflict that led to the <a href=\"/wiki/Struggle_for_the_throne_of_Portugal\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Struggle for the throne of Portugal\">union of the Portuguese and Spanish crowns</a>. The fortress was enlarged towards the end of the 16th century by <a href=\"/wiki/Philip_II_of_Spain\" title=\"Philip II of Spain\">King Philip I</a> (Philip II of Spain), turning it into a typical <a href=\"/wiki/Renaissance\" title=\"Renaissance\">Renaissance</a> <a href=\"/wiki/Citadel\" title=\"Citadel\">citadel</a> with the characteristic flat profile and star-shaped <a href=\"/wiki/Floorplan\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Floorplan\">floorplan</a>. Following the Portuguese restoration in 1640, a dozen bulwarks and redoubts were constructed under the direction of the <a href=\"/wiki/Count_of_Cantanhede\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Count of Cantanhede\">Count of Cantanhede</a>, who oversaw the defences of the Tagus estuary, the gateway to the city of Lisbon.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_14-13\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-14\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>14<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> Of these structures, the <a href=\"/wiki/Citadel_of_Cascais\" title=\"Citadel of Cascais\">citadel of Cascais</a>, which was constructed alongside the fortress of Our Lady of Light, considerably reinforced the strategic defences of the coast.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_14-14\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-14\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>14<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup>\n</p>\n<figure class=\"mw-default-size\" typeof=\"mw:File/Thumb\"><a href=\"/wiki/File:GuimaraesPalace.jpg\" class=\"mw-file-description\"><img src=\"//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5d/GuimaraesPalace.jpg/220px-GuimaraesPalace.jpg\" decoding=\"async\" width=\"220\" height=\"165\" class=\"mw-file-element\" srcset=\"//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5d/GuimaraesPalace.jpg/330px-GuimaraesPalace.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5d/GuimaraesPalace.jpg/440px-GuimaraesPalace.jpg 2x\" data-file-width=\"4032\" data-file-height=\"3024\" /></a><figcaption>View of the <a href=\"/wiki/Pal%C3%A1cio_dos_Condes_de_Castro_Guimar%C3%A3es\" title=\"Pal\u00e1cio dos Condes de Castro Guimar\u00e3es\">Pal\u00e1cio dos Condes de Castro Guimar\u00e3es</a> in Cascais, Portugal.</figcaption></figure>\n<p>In 1755, the great <a href=\"/wiki/1755_Lisbon_earthquake\" title=\"1755 Lisbon earthquake\">Lisbon earthquake</a> destroyed a large portion of the city. Around 1774, the <a href=\"/wiki/Sebasti%C3%A3o_Jos%C3%A9_de_Carvalho_e_Melo,_1st_Marquis_of_Pombal\" title=\"Sebasti\u00e3o Jos\u00e9 de Carvalho e Melo, 1st Marquis of Pombal\">Marquis of Pombal</a>, prime-minister of <a href=\"/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_I_of_Portugal\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Jos\u00e9 I of Portugal\">King Jos\u00e9 I</a>, took protective measures for the commercialisation of the wine of <a href=\"/wiki/Carcavelos\" title=\"Carcavelos\">Carcavelos</a> and established the Royal Factory of Wool in the village, which existed until the early 19th century. During the <a href=\"/wiki/Invasion_of_Portugal_(1807)\" title=\"Invasion of Portugal (1807)\">invasion of Portugal by Napoleonic troops</a> in 1807, the citadel of Cascais was occupied by the <a href=\"/wiki/French_people\" title=\"French people\">French</a>, with <a href=\"/wiki/Andoche_Junot\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Andoche Junot\">General Junot</a> staying some time in the village.\n</p><p>In 1862, the <a href=\"/wiki/Joaquim_Ant%C3%B3nio_Velez_Barreiros\" title=\"Joaquim Ant\u00f3nio Velez Barreiros\">Visconde da Luz</a> built a summer house in Cascais. He and a group of friends also organized the construction of a road from Cascais to <a href=\"/wiki/Oeiras,_Portugal\" title=\"Oeiras, Portugal\">Oeiras</a>, effectively linking Cascais to Lisbon, and also promoted other improvements to the town. As a result of these improvement, <a href=\"/wiki/Lu%C3%ADs_I_of_Portugal\" title=\"Lu\u00eds I of Portugal\">King Lu\u00eds I</a> decided to make Cascais into his summer residence and, from 1870 to 1908, the Portuguese royal family from the <a href=\"/wiki/House_of_Braganza-Saxe-Coburg_and_Gotha\" title=\"House of Braganza-Saxe-Coburg and Gotha\">House of Braganza-Saxe-Coburg and Gotha</a> spent part of the summer in Cascais to enjoy the sea, turning the quiet fishing village into a cosmopolitan address. Thanks to King Lu\u00eds, the citadel was equipped with the country's first <a href=\"/wiki/Electric_light\" title=\"Electric light\">electric lights</a> in 1878. Cascais also benefited from the construction of a better road to Sintra, a <a href=\"/wiki/Bullring\" title=\"Bullring\">bullfight ring</a>, a <a href=\"/wiki/Sporting_Club_of_Cascais\" title=\"Sporting Club of Cascais\">sports club</a>, and improvements to basic infrastructure for the population. Many noble families built impressive mansions in an eclectic style commonly referred to as <i><a href=\"/wiki/Summer_architecture\" title=\"Summer architecture\">summer architecture</a></i>, many of which are still to be seen in the town centre and environs. The first railway arrived in 1889. Another important step in the development of the area was made in the first half of the 20th century with the building of a casino and infrastructure in neighbouring <a href=\"/wiki/Estoril\" title=\"Estoril\">Estoril</a>.\n</p>\n<figure class=\"mw-default-size mw-halign-left\" typeof=\"mw:File/Thumb\"><a href=\"/wiki/File:Cascais_Conde_Castro_Guimaraes.jpg\" class=\"mw-file-description\"><img src=\"//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/db/Cascais_Conde_Castro_Guimaraes.jpg/220px-Cascais_Conde_Castro_Guimaraes.jpg\" decoding=\"async\" width=\"220\" height=\"138\" class=\"mw-file-element\" srcset=\"//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/db/Cascais_Conde_Castro_Guimaraes.jpg/330px-Cascais_Conde_Castro_Guimaraes.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/db/Cascais_Conde_Castro_Guimaraes.jpg/440px-Cascais_Conde_Castro_Guimaraes.jpg 2x\" data-file-width=\"4348\" data-file-height=\"2732\" /></a><figcaption><a href=\"/wiki/Condes_de_Castro_Guimar%C3%A3es_Museum\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Condes de Castro Guimar\u00e3es Museum\">Condes de Castro Guimar\u00e3es Museum</a></figcaption></figure>\n<p>In 1882 Cascais installed one of the first <a href=\"/wiki/Cascais_tide_gauge\" title=\"Cascais tide gauge\">tide gauges</a> in Europe in order to assist with navigation into the port of Lisbon. In 1896, <a href=\"/wiki/Carlos_I_of_Portugal\" title=\"Carlos I of Portugal\">King Carlos I</a>, a lover of all maritime activities, installed in the citadel the first oceanographic laboratory in Portugal. The King himself led a total of 12 scientific expeditions to the coast; these ended in 1908 after his <a href=\"/wiki/Lisbon_Regicide\" title=\"Lisbon Regicide\">assassination in Lisbon</a>.\n</p><p>Due to Portugal's <a href=\"/wiki/Neutrality_(international_relations)\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Neutrality (international relations)\">neutrality</a> in World War II and the town's elegance and royal past, Cascais became home to many of the exiled royal families of Europe, including those of Spain (<a href=\"/wiki/House_of_Bourbon\" title=\"House of Bourbon\">House of Bourbon</a>), Italy (<a href=\"/wiki/House_of_Savoy\" title=\"House of Savoy\">House of Savoy</a>), Hungary and Bulgaria. Their stories are told at the <a href=\"/wiki/Exiles_Memorial_Centre\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Exiles Memorial Centre\">Exiles Memorial Centre</a>.\n</p><p>Nowadays, Cascais and its surroundings are a popular vacation spot for the Portuguese as well as for the international <a href=\"/wiki/Jet_set\" title=\"Jet set\">jet set</a> and regular foreign tourists, all of them drawn by its fine beaches. The town hosts many international events, including sailing and surfing. easily reached from Lisbon by car on the A5 Lisbon-Cascais highway, or alternatively on the scenic \"marginal\" road, as well as by frequent inexpensive commuter trains. Taxis are also a common and inexpensive mode of transport in the area. The city has the ruins of a <a href=\"/wiki/Citadel_of_Cascais\" title=\"Citadel of Cascais\">castle</a>, an <a href=\"/wiki/Casa_das_Hist%C3%B3rias_Paula_Rego\" title=\"Casa das Hist\u00f3rias Paula Rego\">art</a> museum and an <a href=\"/wiki/Museum_of_the_Sea,_Cascais\" title=\"Museum of the Sea, Cascais\">ocean museum</a>, as well as parks and the cobbled streets of the historic centre. The town has many hotels and tourist apartments as well as many good restaurants of varying cost. It is a fine base to use for those visiting Lisbon and its environs who prefer to stay outside of the city.\n</p><p>Cascais ranks 9th in population density and 6th in percentage of population employed among Portuguese municipalities.<sup id=\"cite_ref-24\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-24\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>24<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup>\n</p><p>Cascais is surrounded by popular beaches. <a href=\"/wiki/Guincho_Beach\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Guincho Beach\">Guincho Beach</a> to the northwest is primarily a <a href=\"/wiki/Surfing\" title=\"Surfing\">surfing</a>, <a href=\"/wiki/Windsurfing\" title=\"Windsurfing\">windsurfing</a>, and <a href=\"/wiki/Kitesurfing\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Kitesurfing\">kitesurfing</a> beach because of the prevailing winds and sea swells, while the calm waters of the beaches to the east attract sunbathers. The lush <a href=\"/wiki/Sintra-Cascais_Natural_Park\" title=\"Sintra-Cascais Natural Park\">Sintra</a> mountains to the north are a further attraction. The shoreline to the west has cliffs, attracting tourists who come for the panoramic views of the sea and other natural sights such as the <a href=\"/wiki/Boca_do_Inferno\" title=\"Boca do Inferno\">Boca do Inferno</a>. It is also becoming a popular golf destination, with over 10 <a href=\"/wiki/Golf\" title=\"Golf\">golf courses</a> nearby.\n</p><p>A large <a href=\"/wiki/Cascais_Marina\" title=\"Cascais Marina\">marina</a> with 650 berths was opened in 1999 and has since held many sailing events. It was the official host of the <a href=\"/wiki/2007_ISAF_Sailing_World_Championships\" title=\"2007 ISAF Sailing World Championships\">2007 ISAF Sailing World Championships</a> for dinghies and racing yachts. The municipality also hosts international <a href=\"/wiki/Tennis\" title=\"Tennis\">tennis</a> and motorcycling events and for many years hosted the <a href=\"/wiki/FIA\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"FIA\">FIA</a> F1 Portugal Grand Prix at the <a href=\"/wiki/Aut%C3%B3dromo_Fernanda_Pires_da_Silva\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Aut\u00f3dromo Fernanda Pires da Silva\">Estoril race track</a>. The <a href=\"/wiki/Casino_Estoril\" title=\"Casino Estoril\">Estoril Casino</a> is one of the largest in Europe. Near the casino is the <i>\"Hotel Pal\u00e1cio\"</i> (Palace Hotel), where scenes of the <a href=\"/wiki/James_Bond\" title=\"James Bond\">James Bond</a> movie <i><a href=\"/wiki/On_Her_Majesty%27s_Secret_Service_(film)\" title=\"On Her Majesty&#39;s Secret Service (film)\">On Her Majesty's Secret Service</a></i> were shot.\n</p><p>In 2017 the municipality started charging a small tourist tax, as the city had become one of the most visited destinations in Portugal. It is estimated that around 1.2 million tourists stay in the city's hotels each year (2016).<sup id=\"cite_ref-25\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-25\"><span In its early years it was frequently attended by Portugal's royal family. The Cascais Experimental Theatre was established in 1965 and has presented more than a hundred shows since then. Over the years Cascais has developed several art galleries and museums. These are concentrated in a relatively small area of the town, mainly in parkland. Combined, they are known as The Museum Quarter.<sup id=\"cite_ref-28\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-28\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>28<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> Several occupy large buildings that were formerly private residences and were subsequently taken over and restored by the Municipality. Entrance is either free or for a small fee (usually not more than \u20ac4). The galleries and museums are:\n</p><p><b>Art galleries</b>\n</p>\n<ul><li><b><a href=\"/wiki/Casa_das_Hist%C3%B3rias_Paula_Rego\" title=\"Casa das Hist\u00f3rias Paula Rego\">Casa das Hist\u00f3rias Paula Rego</a>.</b> This is a relatively modern museum devoted to the paintings of <a href=\"/wiki/Paula_Rego\" title=\"Paula Rego\">Paula Rego</a> and her husband <a href=\"/wiki/Victor_Willing\" title=\"Victor Willing\">Victor Willing</a>.</li>\n<li><b><a href=\"/wiki/Cascais_Cultural_Centre\" title=\"Cascais Cultural Centre\">Cascais Cultural Centre</a>.</b> Located on the site of the former convent of Our Lady of Mercy, this colourful building houses rotating exhibitions and also has a small concert hall.<sup id=\"cite_ref-29\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-29\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>29<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup></li>\n<li><b>Casa Duarte Pinto Coelho.</b> The former guardhouse of the Condes de Castro Guimar\u00e3es Palace, this building houses the Duarte Pinto Coelho art collection.<sup id=\"cite_ref-30\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-30\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>30<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup></li>\n<li><b>Cidadela Arts Centre.</b> This occupies a small part of the <a href=\"/wiki/Citadel_of_Cascais\" title=\"Citadel of Cascais\">Citadel of Cascais</a> and offers space for artists to display and sell their work.</li></ul>\n<p><b>Museums</b>\n</p>\n<ul><li>The <b><a href=\"/wiki/Exiles_Memorial_Centre\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Exiles Memorial Centre\">Exiles Memorial Centre</a></b> is located on the first floor of the iconic modernist building that houses the Estoril post office. It is a <a href=\"/wiki/History_museum\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"History museum\">history museum</a> which focuses on the lives of the refugees, exiles, and notables who came through Portugal and Cascais during the <a href=\"/wiki/Second_World_War\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Second World War\">Second World War</a>.</li>\n<li>The <b><a href=\"/wiki/Cascais_Citadel_Palace_Museum\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Cascais Citadel Palace Museum\">Cascais Citadel Palace Museum</a></b> is situated inside the grounds of the Citadel. It was used as the summer residence of the royal family from 1870 until 1908, and was subsequently used as one of the official residences of <a href=\"/wiki/List_of_Presidents_of_Portugal\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"List of Presidents of Portugal\">Portuguese presidents</a>. After extensive restoration it was opened as a museum in 2011, with an emphasis on the role of Portuguese presidents.</li>\n<li><b><a href=\"/wiki/Condes_de_Castro_Guimar%C3%A3es_Museum\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Condes de Castro Guimar\u00e3es Museum\">Condes de Castro Guimar\u00e3es Museum</a>.</b> This was built as an aristocrat's summer residence and became a museum in 1931. The building follows an eclectic architectural style, while the museum includes paintings, furniture, porcelain, jewellery and a <a href=\"/wiki/Neo-Gothic\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Neo-Gothic\">neo-Gothic</a> organ.</li>\n<li><b><a href=\"/wiki/Casa_de_Santa_Maria,_Cascais\" title=\"Casa de Santa Maria, Cascais\">Casa de Santa Maria</a>.</b> This was built for the same person as the building housing the Condes de Castro Guimar\u00e3es Museum. Both are built on the banks of a small sea cove. It was acquired by the Cascais Municipality in October 2004 and is interesting mainly for the design and the wall tiles.</li>\n<li><b>Lighthouse museum.</b> This is built into the <a href=\"/wiki/Santa_Marta_Lighthouse_(Cascais)\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Santa Marta Lighthouse (Cascais)\">Santa Marta Lighthouse</a>, next to the Casa de Santa Maria. Examples of lighthouse lens and other technology can be seen and at certain times the lighthouse can be climbed.</li>\n<li><b><a href=\"/wiki/Casa_Sommer\" title=\"Casa Sommer\">Casa Sommer</a></b> is a distinguished private residence converted into a historical museum. It also houses the Municipal Archives. It is the newest museum in the Quarter, having been opened in 2016.</li>\n<li><b><a href=\"/wiki/Museum_of_the_Sea,_Cascais\" title=\"Museum of the Sea, Cascais\">King D. Carlos Sea Museum</a></b> was inaugurated in 1992. It has a variety of exhibitions reflecting the origins of Cascais as a fishing village.</li>\n<li><b><a href=\"/wiki/Pal%C3%A1cio_dos_Condes_da_Guarda,_Cascais\" title=\"Pal\u00e1cio dos Condes da Guarda, Cascais\">Town museum</a></b> (<a href=\"/wiki/Portuguese_language\" title=\"Portuguese language\">Portuguese</a>: <i lang=\"pt\">Museu da Vila</i>).
