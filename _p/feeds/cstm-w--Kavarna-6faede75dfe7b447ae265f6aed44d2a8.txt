<span lang=\"bg\">\u041a\u0430\u0432\u0430\u0440\u043d\u0430</span> <span class=\"IPA nowrap\" lang=\"bg-Latn-fonipa\"><a href=\"/wiki/Help:IPA/Bulgarian\" title=\"Help:IPA/Bulgarian\">&#91;k\u0250\u02c8varn\u0250&#93;</a></span>, <a href=\"/wiki/Greek_language\" title=\"Greek language\">Greek</a>: <span lang=\"el\">\u039a\u03b1\u03b2\u03ac\u03c1\u03bd\u03b1</span>) is a <a href=\"/wiki/Bulgarian_Black_Sea_Coast\" title=\"Bulgarian Black Sea Coast\">Black Sea coastal</a> town and <a href=\"/wiki/Seaside_resort\" title=\"Seaside resort\">seaside resort</a> in the <a href=\"/wiki/Dobruja\" title=\"Dobruja\">Dobruja</a> region of northeastern <a href=\"/wiki/Bulgaria\" title=\"Bulgaria\">Bulgaria</a>. It lies 42 kilometres (26 miles) northeast of <a href=\"/wiki/Varna,_Bulgaria\" title=\"Varna, Bulgaria\">Varna</a>, 43&#160;km (27&#160;mi) from <a href=\"/wiki/Dobrich\" title=\"Dobrich\">Dobrich</a>, and 43&#160;km (27&#160;mi) south of the border with <a href=\"/wiki/Romania\" title=\"Romania\">Romania</a>. It is the principal town of <a href=\"/wiki/Kavarna_Municipality\" title=\"Kavarna Municipality\">Kavarna Municipality</a>, part of <a href=\"/wiki/Dobrich_Province\" title=\"Dobrich Province\">Dobrich Province</a>. As of December 2009, the town had a population of 11,397.<sup id=\"cite_ref-statistika_1-1\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-statistika-1\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>1<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id=\"cite_ref-population_2-1\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-population-2\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>2<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id=\"cite_ref-pop-stat_3-1\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-pop-stat-3\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>3<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> A little yacht port, a fishing base, a spacious beach and a resort complex exist in the town. The landmark Cape <a href=\"/wiki/Kaliakra\" title=\"Kaliakra\">Kaliakra</a> is located a few kilometers to the east, as is the tiny beachfront resort of <a href=\"/wiki/Rusalka,_Bulgaria\" title=\"Rusalka, Bulgaria\">Rusalka</a>. Three 18-hole <a href=\"/wiki/Golf_courses\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Golf courses\">golf courses</a> with villa communities and marinas are being developed nearby, two of the courses are designed by <a href=\"/wiki/Gary_Player\" title=\"Gary Player\">Gary Player</a> and one by <a href=\"/wiki/Ian_Woosnam\" title=\"Ian Woosnam\">Ian Woosnam</a>.\n</p><p>During the 2000s, the town became famous with the annual <a href=\"/wiki/Kaliakra_Rock_Fest\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Kaliakra Rock Fest\">Kaliakra Rock Fest</a> featuring famous rock bands from around the world.\n</p>\n<meta property=\"mw:PageProp/toc\" />\n<div class=\"mw-heading mw-heading2\"><h2 id=\"Etymology\">Etymology</h2><span class=\"mw-editsection\"><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">[</span><a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Kavarna&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1\" title=\"Edit section: Etymology\"><span>edit</span></a><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">]</span></span></div>\n<p>With the Bulgarian conquest in the 7th century AD, the name <i>Karvuna</i> replaced the similarly-sounding ancient Greek <i>Krounoi</i> as the then name of the neighboring modern town of <a href=\"/wiki/Balchik\" title=\"Balchik\">Balchik</a>; the names <i>Karvunska Hora</i> and <i>Principality of Karvuna</i> were related to that town, which was also mentioned on 14th-century Italian <a href=\"/wiki/Portolan_charts\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Portolan charts\">portolan charts</a> with the Italianized form <i>Carbona</i>.\n</p><p>The name <i>Kavarna</i> as referring to this city was first mentioned in the 15th century; perhaps it was a new name influenced phonetically by the preexisting names of Karvuna (which town was already known as Balchik) and Varna.\n</p>\n<div class=\"mw-heading mw-heading2\"><h2 id=\"Geography\">Geography</h2><span class=\"mw-editsection\"><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">[</span><a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Kavarna&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2\" title=\"Edit section: Geography\"><span>edit</span></a><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">]</span></span></div>\n<p>The lay of the land is flat and the seacoast of Kavarna is 42&#160;km (26&#160;mi) long. Along it there are small beaches, including an artificial one. Gore Chirakman with its almost sheer slopes towers above the seacoast. Remains of stronghold walls, moats, buildings, churches and <a href=\"/wiki/Necropolis\" title=\"Necropolis\">necropolises</a> can be found on the flat <a href=\"/wiki/Plateau\" title=\"Plateau\">plateau</a>. The region is rich in mineral waters.\n</p><p>The area around Kavarna offers opportunities for developing ecotourism and specialized tourism&#160;\u2014 examination and photography of plants, dolphins and various kinds of fish in the coastal waters. The variety of birds attracts tourists from all parts of the world and contributes to Kaliakra and Yaylata becoming one of the most preferred places in Bulgaria for the development of ornithological tourism.\n</p>\n<div class=\"mw-heading mw-heading2\"><h2 id=\"Population\">Population</h2><span class=\"mw-editsection\"><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">[</span><a class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">[</span><a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Kavarna&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4\" title=\"Edit section: History\"><span>edit</span></a><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">]</span></span></div>\n<div class=\"mw-heading mw-heading3\"><h3 id=\"Archaic_and_classical_antiquity\">Archaic and classical antiquity</h3><span class=\"mw-editsection\"><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">[</span><a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Kavarna&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5\" title=\"Edit section: Archaic and classical antiquity\"><span>edit</span></a><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">]</span></span></div>\n<figure typeof=\"mw:File/Thumb\"><a href=\"/wiki/File:Karvuna_1.jpg\" class=\"mw-file-description\"><img src=\"//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/49/Karvuna_1.jpg/250px-Karvuna_1.jpg\" decoding=\"async\" width=\"250\" height=\"190\" class=\"mw-file-element\" srcset=\"//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/49/Karvuna_1.jpg/375px-Karvuna_1.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/49/Karvuna_1.jpg/500px-Karvuna_1.jpg 2x\" data-file-width=\"2800\" data-file-height=\"2128\" /></a><figcaption><a href=\"/wiki/Kaliakra\" title=\"Kaliakra\">Kaliakra</a></figcaption></figure>\n<p>Kavarna's origins lie in a <a href=\"/wiki/Thracians\" title=\"Thracians\">Thracian</a> settlement established between the 9th and 7th centuries BCE.<sup id=\"cite_ref-:0_5-0\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-:0-5\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>5<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> It was later re-founded by <a href=\"/wiki/Greek_colonisation\" title=\"Greek colonisation\">Greek colonists</a> as <b>Byzone</b> (or <i>Bizone</i>) (<a href=\"/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Ancient Greek language\">Ancient Greek</a>: <span lang=\"grc\">\u0392\u03b9\u03b6\u03ce\u03bd\u03b7</span>), a secondary colony of <a href=\"/wiki/Mesembria\" title=\"Mesembria\">Mesembria</a> (modern-day <a href=\"/wiki/Nesebar\" title=\"Nesebar\">Nesebar</a>), a <a href=\"/wiki/Megara\" title=\"Megara\">Megarian</a> colony, likely in the 5th or early 4th century BCE.<sup id=\"cite_ref-:0_5-1\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-:0-5\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>5<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup>\n</p><p>During its early period, Byzone was home to a mixed population, consisting of <a href=\"/wiki/Ancient_Greece\" title=\"Ancient Greece\">Greeks</a>, Thracians, and hybrid Greek-Thracian communities. In the <a href=\"/wiki/Hellenistic_period\" title=\"Hellenistic period\">Hellenistic period</a>, it is believed that some <a href=\"/wiki/Scythians\" title=\"Scythians\">Scythians</a> also settled in the area.<sup id=\"cite_ref-:0_5-2\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-:0-5\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>5<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> By this time, Byzone had grown into an important port and commercial hub, facilitating trade between the local Thracian and Scythian populations and merchants from <a href=\"/wiki/Mainland_Greece\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Mainland Greece\">mainland Greece</a>, <a href=\"/wiki/Anatolia\" title=\"Anatolia\">Asia Minor</a>, and the Aegean islands. Goods such as ceramics, bronze vessels, weaponry, wine, and olive oil were imported, while the region exported grain, timber, and raw materials to cities across the <a href=\"/wiki/Greek_world\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Greek world\">Greek world</a>.<sup id=\"cite_ref-:0_5-3\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-:0-5\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>5<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> During the 3rd and 2nd century BC, the town played an important mediating role between the local <a href=\"/wiki/Thracians\" title=\"Thracians\">Thracian</a> settlements and the Greeks. Despite being unsuitable for wharfing because of its rugged cliffs, this part of the <a href=\"/wiki/Bulgarian_Black_Sea_Coast\" title=\"Bulgarian Black Sea Coast\">Bulgarian Black Sea Coast</a> was an attractive centre due to the fact that the local people produced and traded with high quality grain.\n</p><p>In the 1st century BCE, a disastrous earthquake struck the area, causing significant damage to Byzone. The disaster led to the destruction of much of the town, with a large part of its rocky promontory collapsing into the <a href=\"/wiki/Black_Sea\" title=\"Black Sea\">Black Sea</a>'s waters.<sup id=\"cite_ref-:0_5-4\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-:0-5\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>5<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> According to the leader of the finished first part of the underwater archaeological expedition Kavarna 2005, Asen Salkin, the Roman town of Bizone has sunk two times in the sea. For that evidence the located form skin-divers borders of a sunk residential district of the town of Bizone in Kavarna's coast. For the presence of residential buildings the archaeologists judge by the found ashlars and parts of brick walls. The finds date from the 2nd century AD. According to the leader of the expedition this residential district has nothing in common with the disastrous earthquake from the 1st century BC. The underwater finds evidence for other phenomenon, for which the scientists express only suppositions, such as transgression and regression of the strata. It has started to transgress during the 1st century AD and this has continued to the 2nd century. It is possible that during this period the coast had risen by four metres and one day it suddenly fallen through.\n</p><p>A smaller settlement and port emerged on the site during <a href=\"/wiki/Roman_Empire\" title=\"Roman Empire\">Roman</a> times, but it never regained its former prominence as a major trade center. Even in the late Roman period, the town was unable to recover its earlier status, and remained of lesser importance.<sup id=\"cite_ref-:0_5-5\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-:0-5\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>5<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup>\n</p>\n<div class=\"mw-heading mw-heading3\"><h3 id=\"Medieval_period\">Medieval period</h3><span class=\"mw-editsection\"><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">[</span><a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Kavarna&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6\" title=\"Edit section: Medieval period\"><span>edit</span></a><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">]</span></span></div>\n<figure typeof=\"mw:File/Thumb\"><a href=\"/wiki/File:Kavarna_fortress.png\" class=\"mw-file-description\"><img src=\"//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/91/Kavarna_fortress.png/250px-Kavarna_fortress.png\" decoding=\"async\" width=\"250\" height=\"141\" class=\"mw-file-element\" srcset=\"//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/91/Kavarna_fortress.png/375px-Kavarna_fortress.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/91/Kavarna_fortress.png/500px-Kavarna_fortress.png 2x\" data-file-width=\"2800\" data-file-height=\"1576\" /></a><figcaption>Kavarna fortress plan</figcaption></figure>\n<p>In the 7th century AD the <a href=\"/wiki/Slavs\" title=\"Slavs\">Slavs</a> and <a href=\"/wiki/Asparuh\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Asparuh\">Asparuh</a>'s <a href=\"/wiki/Bulgars\" title=\"Bulgars\">Bulgars</a> destroyed the <a href=\"/wiki/Byzantine\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Byzantine\">Byzantine</a> town and later founded a new settlement, which entered the <a href=\"/wiki/First_Bulgarian_Empire\" title=\"First Bulgarian Empire\">First Bulgarian Empire</a>. In the late Middle Ages the settlement grew and was subject to <a href=\"/wiki/Tatar\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Tatar\">Tatar</a> raids; in the 14th century it became part of the <a href=\"/wiki/Principality_of_Karvuna\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Principality of Karvuna\">Principality of Karvuna</a>, which de facto broke away from the Second Bulgarian Empire under the rule of the <a href=\"/wiki/Despot_(court_title)\" title=\"Despot (court title)\">despots</a> <a href=\"/wiki/Balik_(ruler)\" title=\"Balik (ruler)\">Balik</a> and <a href=\"/wiki/Dobrotitsa\" title=\"Dobrotitsa\">Dobrotitsa</a> of the Bulgarian royal Terter dynasty.<sup id=\"cite_ref-6\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-6\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>6<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id=\"cite_ref-7\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-7\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>7<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> In 1397, the <a href=\"/wiki/Ottoman_Empire\" title=\"Ottoman Empire\">Ottoman Turks</a> nearly destroyed the city, which was abandoned but resettled again and rebuilt by the early 17th century. Its present name was documented for the first time in the early 15th century.\n</p><p>The town was considered an economical and cultural centre during <a href=\"/wiki/Ancient_history\" title=\"Ancient history\">Antiquity</a> and the <a href=\"/wiki/Middle_Ages\" title=\"Middle Ages\">Middle Ages</a> with rich and various remains&#160;\u2013 stronghold walls, early-<a href=\"/wiki/Christianity\" title=\"Christianity\">Christian</a> <a href=\"/wiki/Basilica\" title=\"Basilica\">basilica</a>, <a href=\"/wiki/Medieval\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Medieval\">medieval</a> <a href=\"/wiki/Church_(building)\" title=\"Church (building)\">churches</a>, and public buildings.\n</p><p>Notable works of art, such as coins from different historical epochs, golden adornments, a golden Thracian treasure of applications, have been found in the area.\n</p>\n<div class=\"mw-heading mw-heading3\"><h3 id=\"Modern_period\">Modern period</h3><span class=\"mw-editsection\"><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">[</span><a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Kavarna&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7\" title=\"Edit section: Modern period\"><span>edit</span></a><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">]</span></span></div>\n<figure typeof=\"mw:File/Thumb\"><a href=\"/wiki/File:Kavarna_HDR.jpg\" class=\"mw-file-description\"><img src=\"//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ea/Kavarna_HDR.jpg/300px-Kavarna_HDR.jpg\" decoding=\"async\" width=\"300\" height=\"199\" class=\"mw-file-element\" srcset=\"//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ea/Kavarna_HDR.jpg/450px-Kavarna_HDR.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ea/Kavarna_HDR.jpg/600px-Kavarna_HDR.jpg 2x\" data-file-width=\"1024\" data-file-height=\"680\" /></a><figcaption>View of Kavarna</figcaption></figure><p>Between the 15th and 19th century the town becomes popular under the name Kavarna, as a Christian settlement and port for grain export. From that time remain a <a href=\"/wiki/Turkish_bath\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Turkish bath\">Turkish bath</a>, a medieval necropolis, a bridge, fountains, Christian churches and many inscriptions.\n</p><p>During the <a href=\"/wiki/Russo-Turkish_War_of_1877%E2%80%931878\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Russo-Turkish War of 1877\u20131878\">Russo-Turkish War of 1877\u20131878</a> Kavarna's Christian inhabitants, Bulgarians and <a href=\"/wiki/Gagauz_people\" title=\"Gagauz people\">Gagauz</a> alike, rebelled against the <a href=\"/wiki/Bashi-bazouk\" title=\"Bashi-bazouk\">bashi-bazouks</a> and Circassian hordes in what came to be known as the <a href=\"/wiki/Kavarna_massacre\" title=\"Kavarna massacre\">Kavarna massacre</a> or Kavarna uprising.<sup id=\"cite_ref-8\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-8\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>8<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> After the liberation, the town became part of the <a href=\"/wiki/Principality_of_Bulgaria\" title=\"Principality of Bulgaria\">Principality of Bulgaria</a>.\n</p><p>From the beginning of 20th century Kavarna achieved a rapid progress as an economical and cultural centre. The town renamed <i>Cavarna</i> came under <a href=\"/wiki/Romania\" title=\"Romania\">Romanian</a> rule after the <a href=\"/wiki/Second_Balkan_War\" title=\"Second Balkan War\">Second Balkan War</a> in 1913 and again after the <a href=\"/wiki/First_World_War\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"First World War\">First World War</a> in 1919. This however, was met with resistance by the local Bulgarian population and its <a href=\"/wiki/Internal_Dobrujan_Revolutionary_Organisation\" title=\"Internal Dobrujan Revolutionary Organisation\">Internal Dobrujan Revolutionary Organisation</a>. In 1940 the town was ceded back to Bulgaria by the <a href=\"/wiki/Treaty_of_Craiova\" title=\"Treaty of Craiova\">Treaty of Craiova</a>.\n</p>\n<div class=\"mw-heading mw-heading2\"><h2 id=\"Monuments_and_museums\">Monuments and museums</h2><span class=\"mw-editsection\"><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">[</span><a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Kavarna&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8\" title=\"Edit section: Monuments and museums\"><span>edit</span></a><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">]</span></span></div>\n<div class=\"mw-heading mw-heading3\"><h3 id=\"Dobruja_and_the_Sea_Display\">Dobruja and the Sea Display</h3><span class=\"mw-editsection\"><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">[</span><a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Kavarna&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9\" title=\"Edit section: Dobruja and the Sea Display\"><span>edit</span></a><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">]</span></span></div>\n<p>The Dobruja and the Sea Display is a small maritime museum. It is situated in an approximately restored <a href=\"/wiki/Turkey\" title=\"Turkey\">Turkish</a> bath, the Hamam. It was built in the beginning of the 15th century and represents a massive beehive bath from stone, situated in the beginning of the valley leading to the port, and half a kilometre apart from the town centre. Many stone anchors, amphorae and ceramics found during the underwater archaeological expeditions are exposed there. Coins from different epochs and a golden Thracian treasure can be seen in the Treasure House Hall The ancient and mediaeval colour painting expend our concepts for the ancient navigation around Dobruja's coasts.\n</p>\n<div class=\"mw-heading mw-heading3\"><h3 id=\"Town_Museum\">Town Museum</h3><span class=\"mw-editsection\"><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">[</span><a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Kavarna&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10\" title=\"Edit section: Town Museum\"><span>edit</span></a><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">]</span></span></div>\n<p>The Town Museum can be found in the building of the local library. Materials, revealing the thousand-year old history of the town are displayed in its exposition. Evidence for the life of the people in the region since ancient times are represented here. A model of prehistoric cave-dwellings, many tools, guns, rifles and pistols from the War of Liberation, as well as ethnographic materials&#160;\u2014 clothing, finery, folk-style cloth, are also exhibited\n</p>\n<div class=\"mw-heading mw-heading3\"><h3 id=\"Ethnographic_Museum\">Ethnographic Museum</h3><span class=\"mw-editsection\"><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">[</span><a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Kavarna&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11\" title=\"Edit section: Ethnographic Museum\"><span>edit</span></a><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">]</span></span></div>\n<figure class=\"mw-halign-right\" typeof=\"mw:File/Thumb\"><a href=\"/wiki/File:Dormition_of_the_Theotokos_Church_-_Kavarna.jpg\" class=\"mw-file-description\"><img src=\"//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/61/Dormition_of_the_Theotokos_Church_-_Kavarna.jpg/250px-Dormition_of_the_Theotokos_Church_-_Kavarna.jpg\" decoding=\"async\" width=\"250\" height=\"206\" class=\"mw-file-element\" srcset=\"//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/61/Dormition_of_the_Theotokos_Church_-_Kavarna.jpg/375px-Dormition_of_the_Theotokos_Church_-_Kavarna.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/61/Dormition_of_the_Theotokos_Church_-_Kavarna.jpg/500px-Dormition_of_the_Theotokos_Church_-_Kavarna.jpg 2x\" data-file-width=\"2308\" data-file-height=\"1900\" /></a><figcaption>Dormition of the Theotokos Church.</figcaption></figure>\n<p>The Ethnographic Museum represents an old house from the end of the 19th century that belonged to a rich family. Its interior reveals the customs and culture of the people inhabiting that part of the country; premises typical for Dobruja houses, tools from the culture and the daily round of the people. The building is surrounded by a garden full of mulberry trees, peonies and tulips.\n</p>\n<div class=\"mw-heading mw-heading3\"><h3 id=\"Churches\">Churches</h3><span class=\"mw-editsection\"><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">[</span><a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Kavarna&amp;action=edit&amp;section=12\" title=\"Edit section: Churches\"><span>edit</span></a><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">]</span></span></div>\n<p>There are two churches in the town both located in the town centre. The Church of <a href=\"/wiki/Saint_George\" title=\"Saint George\">Saint George</a> was built in 1836 and the Dormition of the Theotokos Church in 1860. Both churches played an important role during the <a href=\"/wiki/Ottoman_Empire\" title=\"Ottoman Empire\">Ottoman</a> rule and after the Liberation as educational and cultural centers.\n</p>\n<div class=\"mw-heading mw-heading3\"><h3 id=\"The_old_fountains\">The old fountains</h3><span class=\"mw-editsection\"><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">[</span><a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Kavarna&amp;action=edit&amp;section=13\" title=\"Edit section: The old fountains\"><span>edit</span></a><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">]</span></span></div>\n<p>These were 12 spring fountains situated along the valley to the port. Some of them were destroyed and the rest were restored recently. The great amount of spring water forms a small river.\n</p>\n<div class=\"mw-heading mw-heading2\"><h2 id=\"Culture\">Culture</h2><span class=\"mw-editsection\"><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">[</span><a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Kavarna&amp;action=edit&amp;section=14\" title=\"Edit section: Culture\"><span>edit</span></a><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">]</span></span></div>\n<p>Since 2004 the town has turned in one of the centres of musical life in Bulgaria. Kavarna has been the host to several <a href=\"/wiki/Rock_music\" title=\"Rock music\">rock and roll</a> concerts. In 2005 the most popular band to play in the town's stadium was <a href=\"/wiki/Deep_Purple\" title=\"Deep Purple\">Deep Purple</a>. The other notable performers were mostly legends of German rock and <a href=\"/wiki/Heavy_metal_music\" title=\"Heavy metal music\">heavy metal music</a>, such as <a href=\"/wiki/Scorpions_(band)\" title=\"Scorpions (band)\">Scorpions</a>, <a href=\"/wiki/Axel_Rudi_Pell\" title=\"Axel Rudi Pell\">Axel Rudi Pell</a>, <a href=\"/wiki/Gamma_Ray_(band)\" title=\"Gamma Ray (band)\">Gamma Ray</a>, <a href=\"/wiki/Masterplan_(band)\" title=\"Masterplan (band)\">Masterplan</a> and <a href=\"/wiki/Destruction_(band)\" title=\"Destruction (band)\">Destruction</a>. On 27 August 2005 <a href=\"/wiki/Accept_(band)\" title=\"Accept (band)\">Accept</a>, another German band, played here its last show with original lead vocalist <a href=\"/wiki/Udo_Dirkschneider\" title=\"Udo Dirkschneider\">Udo Dirkschneider</a>. There is also a mural of <a href=\"/wiki/Whitesnake\" title=\"Whitesnake\">Whitesnake</a> singer <a href=\"/wiki/David_Coverdale\" title=\"David Coverdale\">David Coverdale</a>.\n</p><p>2006 saw the start of the <a href=\"/wiki/Kaliakra_Rock_Fest\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Kaliakra Rock Fest\">Kaliakra Rock Fest</a> - A Metal festival (renamed to <a href=\"/wiki/Kaliakra_Rock_Fest\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Kaliakra Rock Fest\">Kavarna Rock Fest</a> in 2010) including many metal bands. The same year followed the tradition with concerts of more metal bands as well as some pop and dance musicians.\n</p><p>In 2007, performers included <a href=\"/wiki/Heaven_and_Hell_(band)\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Heaven and Hell (band)\">Heaven and Hell</a>, <a href=\"/wiki/John_Lawton_(musician)\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"John Lawton (musician)\">John Lawton</a> Band, <a href=\"/wiki/Manowar\" title=\"Manowar\">Manowar</a>, <a href=\"/wiki/Mot%C3%B6rhead\" title=\"Mot\u00f6rhead\">Mot\u00f6rhead</a>, <a href=\"/wiki/Robert_Plant\" title=\"Robert Plant\">Robert Plant</a>, and <a href=\"/wiki/Ronnie_James_Dio\" title=\"Ronnie James Dio\">Ronnie James Dio</a>. The next year saw the return of Manowar within <a href=\"/wiki/Kaliakra_Rock_Fest\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Kaliakra Rock Fest\">Kaliakra Rock Fest</a> with a 5-hour attempt to set a world record for the longest heavy metal concert. The next two days <a href=\"/wiki/Alice_Cooper\" title=\"Alice Cooper\">Alice Cooper</a>, <a href=\"/wiki/Slayer\" title=\"Slayer\">Slayer</a> and <a href=\"/wiki/In_Flames\" title=\"In Flames\">In Flames</a> held concerts. In July 2009, <a href=\"/wiki/M%C3%B6tley_Cr%C3%BCe\" title=\"M\u00f6tley Cr\u00fce\">M\u00f6tley Cr\u00fce</a> headlined the rock-fest, with Scorpions, <a href=\"/wiki/Blind_Guardian\" title=\"Blind Guardian\">Blind Guardian</a> and <a href=\"/wiki/Dream_Theater\" title=\"Dream Theater\">Dream Theater</a> also participating. <a href=\"/wiki/Sonata_Arctica\" title=\"Sonata Arctica\">Sonata Arctica</a> also performed in 2008 and 2011.\n</p><p>The 2013 <a href=\"/wiki/Volvo_World_Match_Play_Championship\" title=\"Volvo World Match Play Championship\">Volvo World Match Play Championship</a> was held at the <a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Thracian_Cliffs_Golf_Course&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1\" class=\"new\" title=\"Thracian Cliffs Golf Course (page does not exist)\">Thracian Cliffs Golf Course</a>.\n</p>\n<div class=\"mw-heading mw-heading2\"><h2 id=\"International_relations\">International relations</h2><span class=\"mw-editsection\"><span
