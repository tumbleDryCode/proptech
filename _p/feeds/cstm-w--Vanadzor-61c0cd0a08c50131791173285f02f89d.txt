lang=\"hy\">\u054e\u0561\u0576\u0561\u0571\u0578\u0580</span> <span class=\"IPA nowrap\" lang=\"hy-Latn-fonipa\"><a href=\"/wiki/Help:IPA/Armenian\" title=\"Help:IPA/Armenian\">&#91;v\u0251n\u0251\u02c8d\u0361z\u0254\u027e&#93;</a></span>) is an urban municipal community and the <a href=\"/wiki/List_of_cities_and_towns_in_Armenia\" title=\"List of cities and towns in Armenia\">third largest city</a> in <a href=\"/wiki/Armenia\" title=\"Armenia\">Armenia</a>, serving as the capital of <a href=\"/wiki/Lori_Province\" title=\"Lori Province\">Lori Province</a> in the northern part of the country. It is located about 128 kilometres (80 miles) north of the capital <a href=\"/wiki/Yerevan\" title=\"Yerevan\">Yerevan</a>. As of the 2011 census, the city had a population of 86,199, down from 148,876 reported at the 1979 official census. Currently, the town has a population of approximately 76,000.<sup id=\"cite_ref-Jan2023_1-2\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-Jan2023-1\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>1<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> Vanadzor is the seat of the <a href=\"/wiki/Diocese_of_Gougark\" title=\"Diocese of Gougark\">Diocese of Gougark</a> of the <a href=\"/wiki/Armenian_Apostolic_Church\" title=\"Armenian Apostolic Church\">Armenian Apostolic Church</a>.\n</p>\n<meta property=\"mw:PageProp/toc\" />\n<div class=\"mw-heading mw-heading2\"><h2 id=\"Etymology\">Etymology</h2><span class=\"mw-editsection\"><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">[</span><a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Vanadzor&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1\" title=\"Edit section: Etymology\"><span>edit</span></a><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">]</span></span></div>\n<p>In the official records of the Russian Empire, the city was labelled as <span title=\"Russian-language romanization\"><i lang=\"ru-Latn\">Karaklis Bolshoy</i></span> (<a href=\"/wiki/Russian_language\" title=\"Russian language\">Russian</a>: <span lang=\"ru\">\u041a\u0430\u0440\u0430\u043a\u043b\u0438\u0441\u044a \u0411.</span>, <small><a href=\"/wiki/Literal_translation\" title=\"Literal translation\">lit.</a>&#8201;</small>&#39;Greater Karakilisa&#39;).<sup id=\"cite_ref-:1_3-0\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-:1-3\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>3<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> Following the <a href=\"/wiki/Sovietization_of_Armenia\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Sovietization of Armenia\">Sovietization of Armenia</a>, the city was renamed <span title=\"Armenian-language text\"><i lang=\"hy-Latn\">Martunashen</i></span> (<span title=\"Armenian-language text\"><span lang=\"hy\">\u0544\u0561\u0580\u057f\u0578\u0582\u0576\u0561\u0577\u0565\u0576</span></span>, alternatively <i><span title=\"Armenian-language text\"><i lang=\"hy-Latn\">Martunakan</i></span></i>) in 1926 after Armenian <a href=\"/wiki/Bolshevik\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Bolshevik\">Bolshevik</a> revolutionary <a href=\"/wiki/Alexander_Miasnikian\" title=\"Alexander Miasnikian\">Alexander Miasnikian</a>.<sup id=\"cite_ref-:3_4-0\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-:3-4\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>4<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> On 3 January 1935, it was renamed <span title=\"Armenian-language text\"><i lang=\"hy-Latn\">Kirovakan</i></span>, after the popular Russian Bolshevik leader <a href=\"/wiki/Sergey_Kirov\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Sergey Kirov\">Sergey Kirov</a>. A close associate of Miasnikian and <a href=\"/wiki/Aghasi_Khanjian\" title=\"Aghasi Khanjian\">Aghasi Khanjian</a>, Kirov had been assassinated on December 1 of the previous year. On 25 June 1992, after Armenia gained its independence, Kirovakan was renamed back to its initial name <i>Vanadzor</i> after the <a href=\"/wiki/Vanadzor_River\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Vanadzor River\">Vanadzor River</a> which flows through the city.<sup id=\"cite_ref-:0_5-0\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-:0-5\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>5<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> The name <i>Vanadzor</i> is composed of the Armenian words <span title=\"Armenian-language text\"><i lang=\"hy-Latn\">vank</i></span> (<span title=\"Armenian-language text\"><span lang=\"hy\">\u057e\u0561\u0576\u0584</span></span>, \"<a href=\"/wiki/Monastery\" title=\"Monastery\">monastery</a>\") and <span title=\"Armenian-language text\"><i lang=\"hy-Latn\">dzor</i></span> (<span title=\"Armenian-language text\"><span lang=\"hy\">\u0571\u0578\u0580</span></span>, \"valley\"). Thus, the name of the city means \"valley of the monastery.\"<sup id=\"cite_ref-6\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-6\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>6<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup>\n</p>\n<div class=\"mw-heading mw-heading2\"><h2 id=\"History\">History</h2><span class=\"mw-editsection\"><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">[</span><a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Vanadzor&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2\" title=\"Edit section: History\"><span>edit</span></a><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">]</span></span></div>\n<figure class=\"mw-default-size mw-halign-left\" typeof=\"mw:File/Thumb\"><a href=\"/wiki/File:Khachqars-vanadzor.jpg\" class=\"mw-file-description\"><img src=\"//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/69/Khachqars-vanadzor.jpg/220px-Khachqars-vanadzor.jpg\" decoding=\"async\" width=\"220\" height=\"142\" class=\"mw-file-element\" srcset=\"//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/69/Khachqars-vanadzor.jpg/330px-Khachqars-vanadzor.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/69/Khachqars-vanadzor.jpg/440px-Khachqars-vanadzor.jpg 2x\" data-file-width=\"876\" data-file-height=\"566\" /></a><figcaption>Old <a href=\"/wiki/Khachkar\" title=\"Khachkar\">khachkars</a> in Vanadzor</figcaption></figure>\n<p>The area of present-day Vanadzor has been settled since the <a href=\"/wiki/Bronze_Age\" title=\"Bronze Age\">Bronze Age</a>, based on the tombs and other historic remains found on the nearby hills of <i>Tagavoranist</i> and <i>Mashtots</i>. In antiquity, the area fell into the canton of Tashir of <a href=\"/wiki/Gugark\" title=\"Gugark\">Gugark</a>, the 13th province of the <a href=\"/wiki/Kingdom_of_Armenia_(antiquity)\" title=\"Kingdom of Armenia (antiquity)\">Kingdom of Armenia</a>. Later, the region was ruled by the other Armenian dynasties of the <a href=\"/wiki/Arsacid_dynasty_of_Armenia\" title=\"Arsacid dynasty of Armenia\">Arsacids</a> and the <a href=\"/wiki/Bagratuni_dynasty\" title=\"Bagratuni dynasty\">Bagratunis</a>. The area was part of the <a href=\"/wiki/Kingdom_of_Tashir-Dzoraget\" title=\"Kingdom of Tashir-Dzoraget\">Kingdom of Lori</a> from the 10th century until the beginning of the 12th century. With the invasion of the <a href=\"/wiki/Seljuk_dynasty\" title=\"Seljuk dynasty\">Seljuk Turks</a>, the region came under the rule of the <a href=\"/wiki/Seljuk_Empire\" title=\"Seljuk Empire\">Seljuk Empire</a>. The settlement was called <i>Gharakilisa</i> (meaning <i>black church</i> in Turkic) by the Seljuks possibly as early as the 13th century, the name being taken from the black-stoned Armenian church of the Holy Mother of God on a nearby hill.\n</p>\n<figure class=\"mw-default-size\" typeof=\"mw:File/Thumb\"><a href=\"/wiki/File:Vanadzor2.jpg\" class=\"mw-file-description\"><img src=\"//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/81/Vanadzor2.jpg/220px-Vanadzor2.jpg\" decoding=\"async\" width=\"220\" height=\"141\" class=\"mw-file-element\" srcset=\"//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/81/Vanadzor2.jpg/330px-Vanadzor2.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/81/Vanadzor2.jpg/440px-Vanadzor2.jpg 2x\" data-file-width=\"1000\" data-file-height=\"643\" /></a><figcaption>City center of Vanadzor in 1928</figcaption></figure>\n<p>In 1801, the entire region of Lori became part of the <a href=\"/wiki/Russian_Empire\" title=\"Russian Empire\">Russian Empire</a> along with the <a href=\"/wiki/Georgia_(country)\" title=\"Georgia (country)\">Georgian</a> state. Lori was a strategically important territory for the Russian defensive forces on the border against Persia. In 1826, the settlement of Gharakilisa was entirely destroyed by Hasan Khan during the <a href=\"/wiki/Russo-Persian_War_(1826%E2%80%931828)\" title=\"Russo-Persian War (1826\u20131828)\">Russo-Persian war</a>. In 1828, the Russians founded a new town as a major centre for the deployment of their troops during the <a href=\"/wiki/Russo-Turkish_War_(1828%E2%80%9329)\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Russo-Turkish War (1828\u201329)\">Russo-Turkish War of 1828\u201329</a>. According to <a href=\"/wiki/Khachatur_Abovian\" title=\"Khachatur Abovian\">Khachatur Abovian</a>, the population of Gharakilisa was not more than 600 by the end of the 1820s, mainly consisting of migrants from <a href=\"/wiki/Yerevan\" title=\"Yerevan\">Yerevan</a>. In 1849, it became part of the <a href=\"/wiki/Erivan_Governorate\" title=\"Erivan Governorate\">Erivan Governorate</a> within the <a href=\"/wiki/Russian_Empire\" title=\"Russian Empire\">Russian Empire</a>. Under its new administrative status, Gharakilisa was flooded with many hundreds of migrating Armenian families from <a href=\"/wiki/Kars\" title=\"Kars\">Kars</a>, <a href=\"/wiki/Ardahan\" title=\"Ardahan\">Ardahan</a> and the <a href=\"/wiki/Western_Armenia\" title=\"Western Armenia\">Western Armenian</a> cities of <a href=\"/wiki/Erzurum\" title=\"Erzurum\">Karin</a> (Erzurum) and <a href=\"/wiki/Do%C4%9Fubayaz%C4%B1t\" title=\"Do\u011fubayaz\u0131t\">Daroynk</a> (Do\u011fubeyaz\u0131t).<sup class=\"noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact\" style=\"white-space:nowrap;\">&#91;<i><a href=\"/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed\" title=\"Wikipedia:Citation needed\"><span title=\"This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2020)\">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>\n</p><p>The town enjoyed a considerable uplift through the opening of the railroad towards <a href=\"/wiki/Tbilisi\" title=\"Tbilisi\">Tbilisi</a> in 1899. The vicinity of the town was the site of the <a href=\"/wiki/Battle_of_Karakilisa\" title=\"Battle of Karakilisa\">Battle of Karakilisa</a> in May 1918, when General <a href=\"/wiki/Tovmas_Nazarbekian\" title=\"Tovmas Nazarbekian\">Tovmas Nazarbekian</a>'s outnumbered troops, led by <a href=\"/wiki/Garegin_Nzhdeh\" title=\"Garegin Nzhdeh\">Garegin Nzhdeh</a> successfully defended the region against the invading <a href=\"/wiki/Ottoman_Army_(1861%E2%80%931922)\" title=\"Ottoman Army (1861\u20131922)\">Ottoman Army</a>, pushed them back just a few days after the crucial Armenian victory in the <a href=\"/wiki/Battle_of_Sardarapat\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Battle of Sardarapat\">Battle of Sardarapat</a>, thus allowing the <a href=\"/wiki/First_Republic_of_Armenia\" title=\"First Republic of Armenia\">Republic of Armenia</a> to come into existence.<sup id=\"cite_ref-7\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-7\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>7<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> On the North side of the <a href=\"/wiki/Spitak\" title=\"Spitak\">Spitak</a>-Vanadzor highway, about 2&#160;km (1&#160;mi) west of the city, there is a little shrine in the ruins of a church, the site of a planned monument to that battle.\n</p>\n<figure class=\"mw-default-size mw-halign-left\" 2x\" data-file-width=\"2592\" data-file-height=\"1944\" /></a><figcaption>View of Vanadzor from the southeast</figcaption></figure>\n<p>In 1920, after only 2 years of independence, Armenia fell under <a href=\"/wiki/Soviet_Union\" title=\"Soviet Union\">Soviet</a> rule. The first city development plan for Gharakilisa, initiated by architects <a href=\"/wiki/Karo_Halabyan\" title=\"Karo Halabyan\">Karo Halabyan</a>, Mikayel Mazmanyan and Gevork Kochar, was adopted in 1929\u20131930. Under the new plan, the town was enlarged towards the East and the West. In 1935, the Soviets renamed the city <i>Kirovakan</i> after the Bolshevik leader <a href=\"/wiki/Sergey_Kirov\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Sergey Kirov\">Sergey Kirov</a>. In 1939, a new remodeling plan for the city, created by architects N. Zargaryan and A. Minasyan, was introduced to create an industrial district and a summer-resort area. The centre of the city was redeveloped during the 1950s. The central town square named after Sergey Kirov (now <a href=\"/wiki/Hayk_Square\" title=\"Hayk Square\">Hayk Square</a>) was constructed along with the surrounding government and administrative buildings.<sup id=\"cite_ref-8\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-8\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>8<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup>\n</p><p>After Armenia gained its independence, Kirovakan was renamed <i>Vanadzor</i> after the <a href=\"/wiki/Vanadzor_(river)\" title=\"Vanadzor (river)\">Vanadzor River</a> that flows through the city.\n</p><p>Vanadzor, along with <a href=\"/wiki/Gyumri\" title=\"Gyumri\">Gyumri</a> and <a href=\"/wiki/Spitak\" title=\"Spitak\">Spitak</a> (25&#160;km (16&#160;mi) west), suffered a considerable amount of damage from the <a href=\"/wiki/1988_Armenian_earthquake\" title=\"1988 Armenian earthquake\">1988 earthquake</a> when 564 residents died in the city. Unlike the two other cities, the majority of buildings in Vanadzor were unscathed from the earthquake.\n</p>\n<div class=\"mw-heading mw-heading2\"><h2 id=\"Geography_and_climate\">Geography and climate</h2><span class=\"mw-editsection\"><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">[</span><a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Vanadzor&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3\" title=\"Edit section: Geography and climate\"><span>edit</span></a><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">]</span></span></div>\n<figure class=\"mw-default-size\" typeof=\"mw:File/Thumb\"><a href=\"/wiki/File:Forests_near_Vanadzor.jpg\" class=\"mw-file-description\"><img src=\"//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2d/Forests_near_Vanadzor.jpg/220px-Forests_near_Vanadzor.jpg\" decoding=\"async\" width=\"220\" height=\"165\" class=\"mw-file-element\" srcset=\"//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2d/Forests_near_Vanadzor.jpg/330px-Forests_near_Vanadzor.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2d/Forests_near_Vanadzor.jpg/440px-Forests_near_Vanadzor.jpg 2x\" data-file-width=\"2048\" data-file-height=\"1536\" /></a><figcaption>Forests around Vanadzor</figcaption></figure>\n<p>Vanadzor, the capital of Lori Province, is located 128&#160;km (80&#160;mi) north of <a href=\"/wiki/Yerevan\" title=\"Yerevan\">Yerevan</a> and 64&#160;km (40&#160;mi) east of <a href=\"/wiki/Gyumri\" title=\"Gyumri\">Gyumri</a>.\n</p><p>At an average height of 1,350 metres (4,430 feet) <a href=\"/wiki/Above_sea_level\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Above sea level\">above sea level</a>, Vanadzor is located in the valley of <a href=\"/wiki/Pambak_River\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Pambak River\">Pambak River</a>, on the point where the rivers of <a href=\"/wiki/Tandzut_River\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Tandzut River\">Tandzut</a> and <a href=\"/wiki/Vanadzor_River\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Vanadzor River\">Vanadzor</a> join the Pambak river. The city is surrounded by the over 2,500-metre-high (8,200-foot) mountains of Bazum and Pambak. The southern and eastern regions are densely forested while to the north and west are only covered with bushes and plants.\n</p><p>The climate of Vanadzor is characterized by warm summers and cold winters. The average temperature in winter is \u22128&#160;\u00b0C (17.6&#160;\u00b0F), while in summer it reaches up to 15&#160;\u00b0C (59&#160;\u00b0F). Vanadzor's climate is classified as <a href=\"/wiki/Warm-summer_humid_continental\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Warm-summer humid continental\">warm summer humid continental</a> (<a href=\"/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification\" title=\"K\u00f6ppen climate classification\">K\u00f6ppen</a>: <i>Dfb</i>). The amount of <a href=\"/wiki/Precipitation\" title=\"Precipitation\">precipitation</a> is around 570&#160;mm (22.4 inches).\n</p><p>The Vanadzor Botanical Garden located to the southwest of the city is one of the 5 botanical parks within the Republic of Armenia.\n</p>\n<div>\n<table class=\"wikitable mw-collapsible\" style=\"width:auto; text-align:center; line-height:1.2em;\">\n\n<tbody><tr>\n<th href=\"/wiki/Yerevan_Governorate\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Yerevan Governorate\">Yerevan Governorate</a>.\n</p><p>The population of Kirovakan-Vanadzor increased significantly after <a href=\"/wiki/World_War_II\" title=\"World War II\">World War II</a>, when <a href=\"/wiki/Joseph_Stalin\" title=\"Joseph Stalin\">Stalin</a> allowed open immigration to the <a href=\"/wiki/Armenian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic\" title=\"Armenian Soviet Socialist Republic\">Armenian Soviet Socialist Republic</a> with the promise of a better life for the arriving Armenians in the historical homeland. Armenians from different parts of <a href=\"/wiki/Europe\" title=\"Europe\">Europe</a> and the <a href=\"/wiki/Middle_East\" title=\"Middle East\">Middle East</a>, most of them survivors of the <a href=\"/wiki/Armenian_genocide\" title=\"Armenian genocide\">Armenian genocide</a>, immigrated and settled in Kirovakan.<sup class=\"noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact\" style=\"white-space:nowrap;\">&#91;<i><a href=\"/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed\" title=\"Wikipedia:Citation needed\"><span title=\"This claim needs references to reliable sources. (June 2022)\">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> Due to the harsh conditions imposed by <a href=\"/wiki/Soviet_intelligence\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Soviet intelligence\">Soviet intelligence</a> and the <a href=\"/wiki/Ministry_of_Internal_Affairs_(Soviet_Union)\" title=\"Ministry of Internal Affairs (Soviet Union)\">Ministry of Interior</a>, many of the immigrants were sent to <a href=\"/wiki/Labor_camp\" title=\"Labor camp\">labor camps</a> in <a href=\"/wiki/Siberia\" title=\"Siberia\">Siberia</a> and elsewhere. The reason for this treatment towards the Armenian immigrants was because almost all were more educated than the local community, spoke more than 3 languages, and were open followers of the <a href=\"/wiki/Armenian_Apostolic_Church\" title=\"Armenian Apostolic Church\">Armenian Apostolic Church</a>, something that was considered a threat to the national security by the Stalinist government.<sup class=\"noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact\" style=\"white-space:nowrap;\">&#91;<i><a href=\"/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed\" title=\"Wikipedia:Citation needed\"><span title=\"This claim needs references to reliable sources. (May 2016)\">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> Despite the difficulties, more Western Armenians immigrated to Kirovakan during the following years. However, many of the immigrants left the city and returned to their diaspora countries, including <a href=\"/wiki/Cyprus\" title=\"Cyprus\">Cyprus</a>, <a href=\"/wiki/Lebanon\" title=\"Lebanon\">Lebanon</a>, <a href=\"/wiki/Syria\" title=\"Syria\">Syria</a>, <a href=\"/wiki/Greece\" title=\"Greece\">Greece</a>, <a href=\"/wiki/France\" title=\"France\">France</a> and the <a href=\"/wiki/United_States\" title=\"United States\">United States</a>. Nowadays, approximately 2,000 Western Armenian settlers are living in Vanadzor, most of whom are still using the Western Armenian dialect in daily life.<sup class=\"noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact\" style=\"white-space:nowrap;\">&#91;<i><a href=\"/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed\" title=\"Wikipedia:Citation needed\"><span title=\"This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2020)\">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>\n</p><p>While the majority of Vanadzor are ethnic Armenians, there are also small communities of Russian, Ukrainian and Greek Despite dating back to the medieval period, the current building was reconstructed in 1841.<sup id=\"cite_ref-13\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-13\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>13<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup></li>\n<li>Church of the Nativity of Blessed Virgin Mary, opened in 1895, is serving the city's small Russian Orthodox community.<sup id=\"cite_ref-14\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-14\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>14<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup></li>\n<li>Surp Sarkis Church of Vanadzor, opened in 2000, is located near the prelacy building.</li>\n<li>Saint Gregory of Narek Cathedral, opened in 2005, is named after Saint <a href=\"/wiki/Gregory_of_Narek\" title=\"Gregory of Narek\">Gregory of Narek</a> to commemorate the 1000th anniversary of his <b>Book of Lamentations</b>. It is currently the seat of the <a href=\"/wiki/Diocese_of_Gougark\" title=\"Diocese of Gougark\">Diocese of Gougark</a> of the Armenian Apostolic Church</li>\n<li><a href=\"/wiki/Holy_Children_Chapel,_Vanadzor\" title=\"Holy Children Chapel, Vanadzor\">Holy Children Chapel</a> opened in the Tsitsernak children's camp of Vanadzor in 2006.</li></ul>\n<p>Many residents of Vanadzor are traditionally members of the <a href=\"/wiki/Armenian_Evangelical_Church\" title=\"Armenian Evangelical Church\">Armenian Evangelical Church</a>, which was first established by arriving Protestant Western Armenians in 1946. Although at that time any religious activity was punishable by law, followers of the Evangelical tradition continued practicing their faith and gathered secretly in basements to avoid deportation to Siberia. Particularly Armenians of <a href=\"/wiki/Musa_Dagh\" title=\"Musa Dagh\">Musa Dagh</a> (Musa Ler) became pioneers of Evangelical faith and adherence to their traditions despite continuous threats by the police and the neighborhood watch.\n</p>\n<div class=\"mw-heading mw-heading2\"><h2 id=\"Culture\">Culture</h2><span class=\"mw-editsection\"><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">[</span><a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Vanadzor&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7\" title=\"Edit section: Culture\"><span>edit</span></a><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">]</span></span></div>\n<figure class=\"mw-default-size\" typeof=\"mw:File/Thumb\"><a href=\"/wiki/File:Lori_-_Vanadzor_14.jpg\" class=\"mw-file-description\"><img src=\"//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/06/Lori_-_Vanadzor_14.jpg/220px-Lori_-_Vanadzor_14.jpg\" decoding=\"async\" width=\"220\" height=\"124\" class=\"mw-file-element\" srcset=\"//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/06/Lori_-_Vanadzor_14.jpg/330px-Lori_-_Vanadzor_14.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/06/Lori_-_Vanadzor_14.jpg/440px-Lori_-_Vanadzor_14.jpg 2x\" data-file-width=\"4128\" data-file-height=\"2322\" /></a><figcaption>Vanadzor House of Culture named after <a href=\"/wiki/Charles_Aznavour\" title=\"Charles Aznavour\">Charles Aznavour</a></figcaption></figure>\n<p>Vanadzor has several archaeological sites including the nearby Mashtots hill which is home to many remains from the 4th millennium BC. The Lori-Pambak archaeological museum opened in 1938 is dedicated to the history and ethnography of Gugark region. It currently has a collection of around 34,000 pieces and artifacts.\n</p><p>Vanadzor is the cultural center of Lori Province and northeastern Armenia. The Vanadzor House of Culture named after Charles Aznavour is one of the largest centers in Armenia. The Eduard Kzartmyan school of music is a prominent art school founded in 1934. The Vanadzor fine art museum is operating since 1974, while the house-museum of writer <a href=\"/wiki/Stepan_Zoryan\" title=\"Stepan Zoryan\">Stepan Zoryan</a> is operating since 1990.<sup id=\"cite_ref-15\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-15\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>15<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup>\n</p><p>The city is home to many theatres including the <i>Bohem Chamber Theatre of Vanadzor</i>, <i>Vanadzor Musical Theatre</i>, <i>Vanadzor Drama Theatre named after Hovhannes Abelyan</i>, and <i>Vanadzor Puppet Theatre</i>. Other famous art ensembles of Vanadzor include Horevel Folke Dance Ensemble (founded in 1958), Vanadzor Chamber Music Choir (1989), and Vanadzor Chamber Orchestra (1993).\n</p><p>Vanadzor has also produced many famous <a href=\"/wiki/Hard_rock\" title=\"Hard rock\">hard rock</a> bands including <i>Lousnelius</i>, <i>Lav Eli</i> and <i>Vordan Karmir</i>.\n</p><p>As of 2016<sup class=\"plainlinks The largest garment manufacturers of the city are the \"Dav-Gar\" garment factory founded in 1962, the Darbbag's Sewing Factory founded in 1977 (privatized in 1996), the \"Sarton\" Sewing Factory founded in 1990, \"Ruz-Dan\" garment factory founded in 2000. The largest chemical plants of Vanadzor include the \"Vanadzor ChimProm\" founded in 1929, the \"Gipk\" enterprise for polymeric materials founded in 1967, and the \"Gary Group\" for polymeric materials founded in 1998. The currently defunct football club <a href=\"/wiki/Lori_Vanadzor\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Lori Vanadzor\">Lori Vanadzor</a> founded in 1936, was one of the oldest football clubs in Armenia. After the independence of Armenia, the team represented the city in domestic competitions until 2006 when was dissolved due to financial difficulties.\n</p><p>The largest sport venue in the city is the <a href=\"/wiki/Lori_Stadium\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Lori Stadium\">Lori Stadium</a> with a capacity of 5,000 spectators. It is mainly used for football matches. A renovation plan was scheduled in order to redevelop the stadium to meet the requirements of the <a href=\"/wiki/UEFA\" title=\"UEFA\">UEFA</a>.<sup id=\"cite_ref-22\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-22\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>22<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup>\n</p><p>The <a href=\"/wiki/Football_Federation_of_Armenia\" title=\"Football Federation of Armenia\">FFA</a> <a href=\"/wiki/Vanadzor_Football_Academy\" title=\"Vanadzor Football Academy\">Vanadzor Football Academy</a> opened its doors on 29 October 2016.<sup id=\"cite_ref-23\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-23\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>23<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id=\"cite_ref-24\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-24\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>24<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> It is home to 3 natural grass and 1 artificial turf regular-sized pitches.\n</p><p>In 2017, a new football club named <a href=\"/wiki/Lori_FC\" title=\"Lori FC\">Lori</a> was founded by Tovmas Grigoryan; a native businessman of Vanadzor. The club made its debut at professional football through the <a href=\"/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_Armenian_First_League\" title=\"2017\u201318 Armenian First League\">2017\u201318 Armenian First League</a> competition.\n</p>\n<div class=\"mw-heading mw-heading3\"><h3 id=\"Other_sports\">Other sports</h3><span class=\"mw-editsection\"><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">[</span><a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Vanadzor&amp;action=edit&amp;section=15\" title=\"Edit section: Other sports\"><span>edit</span></a><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">]</span></span></div>\n<figure class=\"mw-default-size\" typeof=\"mw:File/Thumb\"><a href=\"/wiki/File:Lori,_Vanadzor_ski_resort.jpg\" class=\"mw-file-description\"><img src=\"//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e1/Lori%2C_Vanadzor_ski_resort.jpg/220px-Lori%2C_Vanadzor_ski_resort.jpg\" decoding=\"async\" width=\"220\" height=\"147\" class=\"mw-file-element\" srcset=\"//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e1/Lori%2C_Vanadzor_ski_resort.jpg/330px-Lori%2C_Vanadzor_ski_resort.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e1/Lori%2C_Vanadzor_ski_resort.jpg/440px-Lori%2C_Vanadzor_ski_resort.jpg 2x\" data-file-width=\"1600\" data-file-height=\"1067\" /></a><figcaption>Vanadzor ski resort</figcaption></figure>\n<p><a href=\"/wiki/Futsal\" title=\"Futsal\">Futsal</a> is among the popular team sports in the city. The <a href=\"/wiki/Vanadzor_State_University\" title=\"Vanadzor State University\">VSU</a> futsal team plays at the <a href=\"/wiki/Armenian_Futsal_Premier_League\" title=\"Armenian Futsal Premier League\">Armenian Futsal Premier League</a>. The Armenia Sports Arena of Vanadzor is the home venue of the team.\n</p><p><a href=\"/wiki/Handball\" title=\"Handball\">Handball</a> and <a href=\"/wiki/Volleyball\" title=\"Volleyball\">volleyball</a> are also popular in Vanadzor. Many sport schools operate in the city under the supervision of the municipality, including the schools of swimming, athletics, weightlifting, chess, artistic gymnastics, table tennis and badminton.\n</p><p>Winter sports are also practiced in Lori Province.
