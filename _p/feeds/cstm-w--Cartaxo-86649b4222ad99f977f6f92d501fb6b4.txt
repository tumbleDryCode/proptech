A Roman road, crossing Alenquer (Lerabriga), connected ancient <a href=\"/wiki/Olissipo\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Olissipo\">Olissipo</a> (Lisbon) to Santar\u00e9m (Scallabis) through the territory of Cartaxo.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMCartaxo_4-0\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMCartaxo-4\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>4<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> Yet, before the Romans, other civilizations settled in the region, establishing <a href=\"/wiki/Castro_culture\" title=\"Castro culture\">castros</a> in Vila Nova de S\u00e3o Pedro, Vale do Tejo or in the areas of Muge.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMCartaxo_4-1\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMCartaxo-4\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>4<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup>\n</p><p>Situated in the plains of the Ribatejo, Cartaxo was a battleground between Muslim and the Christians. Due to its proximity to Santar\u00e9m, it was one of the centres disputed between Muslim and Christian forces for years, resulting in the destruction of Cartaxo.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMCartaxo_4-2\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMCartaxo-4\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>4<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> King <a href=\"/wiki/Sancho_II_of_Portugal\" title=\"Sancho II of Portugal\">Sancho II of Portugal</a> found it necessary to repopulate the area, since it was located in a privileged position with fertile lands.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMCartaxo_4-3\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMCartaxo-4\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>4<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> He, therefore, assigned the land of Cartaxinho (today Ribeira do Cartaxo) to Pedro Pacheco, who was responsible for constructing a shelter for the poor, a task that neither Pedro Pacheco nor his descendants would accomplish.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMCartaxo_4-4\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMCartaxo-4\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>4<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup>\n</p><p>Oral tradition suggests that the name <i>Cartaxo</i> was given to the lands by Queen <a href=\"/wiki/Elizabeth_of_Portugal\" title=\"Elizabeth of Portugal\">Elizabeth of Portugal</a> who, on a journey to the Monastery of Almoster, stopped to rest and quench her thirst. Alongside a spring, she was surprised by the beautiful song of an unfamiliar bird.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMCartaxo_4-5\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMCartaxo-4\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>4<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> Asking one of the peasants about the songbird, the serf responded that the song came from a bird of the kind called <i>cartaxos</i> or <i>cartaxinhos</i>. Enchanted by the place, the Queen ordered that the land, then called <i>Lugar da Fonte</i>, receive the name <i>Lugar do Cartaxo</i>.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMCartaxo_4-6\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMCartaxo-4\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>4<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> It is unclear whether there is any truth to this story.\n</p><p>In 1312, Cartaxo received its first <a href=\"/wiki/Foral\" title=\"Foral\">foral</a> (<i>charter</i>), signed in Leiria by King <a href=\"/wiki/Dinis_of_Portugal\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Dinis of Portugal\">Dinis of Portugal</a>, confirmed in 1487 by King <a href=\"/wiki/John_II_of_Portugal\" title=\"John II of Portugal\">John II</a> (in Santar\u00e9m), and in 1496 by <a href=\"/wiki/Manuel_I_of_Portugal\" title=\"Manuel I of Portugal\">Manuel</a> (also in Santar\u00e9m). The first foral, issued by Dinis on 21 March 1312, was sent to his vassal, Garcia Martins, originating the foundation of the locality of Cartaxo.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMCartaxo_4-7\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMCartaxo-4\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>4<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> In this act, Dinis exempted the region from taxes for those who grew and cultivated grapes during the first five years of their tenure. Settlers who accepted these land rights were required in return to present each year an eighth of their produce in wine and linen.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMCartaxo_4-8\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMCartaxo-4\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>4<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup>\n</p><p>The historical importance of the municipality is supported by events of the <a href=\"/wiki/Battle_of_Ourique\" title=\"Battle of Ourique\">Battle of Ourique</a>, which were probably linked to Vila Ch\u00e3 de Ourique (1139), a concession of foral to Pont\u00e9vel, by King <a href=\"/wiki/Sancho_I_of_Portugal\" title=\"Sancho I of Portugal\">Sancho</a> (1194) and the existence of the Royal Palaces of Valada (1361-1365).<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMCartaxo_4-9\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMCartaxo-4\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>4<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup>\n</p><p>In the middle of the 19th century, Cartaxo was one of the more populated areas of Estremadura, in the Comarca of Santar\u00e9m, with just over 200 inhabitants.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMCartaxo_4-10\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMCartaxo-4\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>4<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> On the death of King <a href=\"/wiki/John_IV_of_Portugal\" title=\"John IV of Portugal\">John IV of Portugal</a>, his testament granted several \"honours\" to his daughter (in 1656).<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMCartaxo_4-11\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMCartaxo-4\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>4<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> On 10 December 1815, by royal decree from Rio de Janeiro, King John VI granted it administrative independence, followed up on 21 June 1995 with its elevation to the status of city.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMCartaxo_4-12\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMCartaxo-4\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>4<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup>\n</p><p>At the end of the 19th century, owing to the growth of technological innovations, Cartaxo became the centre of production of the typical wine of the Valley of the Tejo, much of which was sent by ship to Lisbon.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMCartaxo_4-13\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMCartaxo-4\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>4<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup>\n</p>\n<div class=\"mw-heading mw-heading2\"><h2 id=\"Physical_geography\">Physical geography</h3><span class=\"mw-editsection\"><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">[</span><a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Cartaxo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3\" title=\"Edit section: Physical geography\"><span>edit</span></a><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">]</span></span></div>\n<p>Cartaxo is located 50 kilometres (31&#160;mi) from <a href=\"/wiki/Lisbon\" title=\"Lisbon\">Lisbon</a> (its airport is the primary international gateway), and 15 kilometres (9.3&#160;mi) from the district capital, <a href=\"/wiki/Santar%C3%A9m,_Portugal\" title=\"Santar\u00e9m, Portugal\">Santar\u00e9m</a>, served by many roadways and rail-lines. Direct access by the A1 motorway, its variant the Estrada Nacional E.N. 365\u20132, to the intersection of the A1 at <a href=\"/wiki/Aveiras_de_Cima\" title=\"Aveiras de Cima\">Aveiras de Cima</a>, also the E.N.3, that crosses the municipality, connecting the area between Carregado and Santar\u00e9m.\n</p><p>The fields, town and flat-lands, with the river at their feet, make Cartaxo a rich landscape. The municipality is marked by agricultural fields, primarily vineyards, that occupy a large portion of the land. Vine cultivation and wine production has always been linked to the municipality, earning it the title <i>Wine Capital</i> of the <a href=\"/wiki/Ribatejo\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Ribatejo\">Ribatejo</a>.\n</p>\n<div class=\"mw-heading mw-heading3\"><h3 id=\"Human_geography\">Human geography</h3><span class=\"mw-editsection\"><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">[</span><a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Cartaxo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4\" title=\"Edit section: Human geography\"><span>edit</span></a><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">]</span></span></div>\n<p>Situated in the heart of the <a href=\"/wiki/Ribatejo\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Ribatejo\">Ribatejo</a>, the municipality of Cartaxo occupies an area of approximately 160 square kilometres (62&#160;sq&#160;mi), comprised from the <a href=\"/wiki/Freguesia_(Portugal)\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Freguesia (Portugal)\">civil parishes</a> of Cartaxo, Ereira, Lapa, Pont\u00e9vel, Valada, Vale da Pedra, Vale da Pinta and Vila Ch\u00e3 de Ourique.\n</p><p>Administratively, the municipality is divided into 6 civil parishes (<i><a href=\"/wiki/Freguesia_(Portugal)\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Freguesia (Portugal)\">freguesias</a></i>):<sup id=\"cite_ref-5\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-5\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>5<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup>\n</p>\n<ul><li>Cartaxo e Vale da Pinta</li>\n<li>Ereira e Lapa</li>\n<li>Pont\u00e9vel</li>\n<li>Valada</li>\n<li>Vale da Pedra</li>\n<li>Vila Ch\u00e3 de Ourique</li></ul>\n<p>The municipality is served by the <i>Linha Ferrovi\u00e1ria do Norte</i>, with the station at Setil, the most important: it is part of the national rail network connection between the north and south. The rail stations in Santana and Reguengo are also points of departure and ingress to Cartaxo, permitting a level of regional mobility.\n</p>\n<div class=\"mw-heading mw-heading4\"><h4
