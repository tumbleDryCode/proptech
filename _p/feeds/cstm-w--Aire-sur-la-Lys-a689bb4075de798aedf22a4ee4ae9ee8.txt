class=\"mw-editsection\"><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">[</span><a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Aire-sur-la-Lys&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1\" title=\"Edit section: Geography\"><span>edit</span></a><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">]</span></span></div>\n<p>The commune is located 16 kilometres (10&#160;mi) southeast of <a href=\"/wiki/Saint-Omer\" title=\"Saint-Omer\">Saint-Omer</a>, at the junction of the N43 with several departmental roads, by the banks of the <a href=\"/wiki/Leie\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Leie\">Leie</a> (<a href=\"/wiki/French_language\" title=\"French language\">French</a>: <i>Lys</i>) and the <a href=\"/wiki/Laquette\" title=\"Laquette\">Laquette</a> rivers.\n</p>\n<div class=\"mw-heading mw-heading2\"><h2 id=\"History\">History</h2><span class=\"mw-editsection\"><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">[</span><a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Aire-sur-la-Lys&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2\" title=\"Edit section: History\"><span>edit</span></a><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">]</span></span></div>\n<p>Aire-sur-la-Lys is mentioned for the first time in 857. It developed around a fort or <a href=\"/wiki/Castrum\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Castrum\">castrum</a> built by <a href=\"/wiki/Baldwin_II,_Count_of_Flanders\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Baldwin II, Count of Flanders\">Baldwin II, Count of Flanders</a> in response to the <a href=\"/wiki/Normans\" title=\"Normans\">Norman</a> invasions. More growth followed with the establishment of the <a href=\"/wiki/Collegiate_church\" title=\"Collegiate church\">Collegiate church</a> of Saint-Pierre by <a href=\"/wiki/Baldwin_V,_Count_of_Flanders\" title=\"Baldwin V, Count of Flanders\">Baldwin V, Count of Flanders</a>.\n</p><p>The town was laid siege ten times between 1127 and 1710. It was separated from the <a href=\"/wiki/County_of_Flanders\" title=\"County of Flanders\">County of Flanders</a> and attached to the <a href=\"/wiki/County_of_Artois\" title=\"County of Artois\">County of Artois</a> in 1196. Subsequently, ruled by the <a href=\"/wiki/Burgundy_(region)\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Burgundy (region)\">Burgundians</a> then by the <a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Spanish_and_Austrian_Habsburgs&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1\" class=\"new\" title=\"Spanish and Austrian Habsburgs (page does not exist)\">Spanish and Austrian Habsburgs</a>.\n</p><p>The town was besieged in 1676 by <a href=\"/wiki/S%C3%A9bastien_Le_Prestre_de_Vauban\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"S\u00e9bastien Le Prestre de Vauban\">Vauban</a><sup id=\"cite_ref-4\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-4\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>4<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> and retaken for France, although it remained a Habsburg possession until 14 April 1713, when, by the <a href=\"/wiki/Treaty_of_Utrecht\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Treaty of Utrecht\">Treaty of Utrecht</a>, it finally became a part of France.\n</p><p><a href=\"/wiki/Vauban\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Vauban\">Vauban</a>\u2019s stronghold, which was a strategic position, was dismantled in 1893.\n</p>\n<div class=\"mw-heading class=\"mw-editsection\"><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">[</span><a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Aire-sur-la-Lys&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4\" title=\"Edit section: Sights\"><span>edit</span></a><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">]</span></span></div>\n<div class=\"mw-heading mw-heading3\"><h3 id=\"Collegiate_Church_of_Saint-Pierre\">Collegiate Church of Saint-Pierre</h3><span class=\"mw-editsection\"><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">[</span><a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Aire-sur-la-Lys&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5\" title=\"Edit section: Collegiate Church of Saint-Pierre\"><span>edit</span></a><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">]</span></span></div>\n<p>The church is one of the most important monuments, with its imposing style, in <a href=\"/wiki/Artois\" title=\"Artois\">Artois</a>. It has all the characteristics of a cathedral, but in the absence of a <a href=\"/wiki/Bishop\" title=\"Bishop\">bishop</a>, it cannot claim that title.\nWitness to the splendour of the city, the collegiate church of Saint-Pierre is of <a href=\"/wiki/Romanesque_architecture\" title=\"Romanesque architecture\">Romanesque</a> style. Built between 1492 and the eighteenth century, one can see both on the inside and the outside of the building carving that details the 400 years progress. It has been the parish church since 1802.\nThe tower, completed in 1624, collapsed soon after. Ten years later, the damage was repaired. The top of the tower was again restored between May 2005 and April 2007. The ground floor, the nave, choir and high arches are essentially Gothic.\n</p>\n<div style=\"clear:left;\" class=\"\"></div>\n<p>The dimensions of the building are impressive:\n</p>\n<ul><li>Total external length: 105 metres</li>\n<li>Total external width: 40 metres</li>\n<li>Width of the central nave: 10 metres</li>\n<li>Height of the tower: 66 metres</li></ul>\n<p>It was classified a historical monument in 1862.\n</p>\n<div class=\"mw-heading mw-heading3\"><h3 id=\"Le_Bailliage\">Le Bailliage</h3><span class=\"mw-editsection\"><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">[</span><a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Aire-sur-la-Lys&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6\" title=\"Edit section: Le Bailliage\"><span>edit</span></a><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">]</span></span></div>\n<p>A Flemish Renaissance style building which was built between 1595 and 1600,  as guardrooms for the militia of the city. It is commonly known as the Le Bailliage (<a href=\"/wiki/Bailiwick\" title=\"Bailiwick\">Bailiwick</a>), because at various times between 1634 and 1789, it served as the seat of the court bailiffs.\nIn 1595, the mayor, one Jacques de Caverel had obtained permission from <a href=\"/wiki/Brussels\" title=\"Brussels\">Brussels</a> to raise taxes on beer and wine for the construction of the guardhouse on the city marketplace and the monument was inaugurated on 22 November 1600.\nThe building itself is an irregular quadrilateral of 125m square, with 3 facades giving out onto the Grande Place, Rue d\u2019Arras and what was then the Rue des Cuisiniers. The architect, Pierre Framery was inspired by the former Hotel de Ville at <a href=\"/wiki/Amsterdam\" title=\"Amsterdam\">Amsterdam</a>. Many restorations have been necessary over the centuries.\nThe Bailliage has undergone various uses: guardhouse, courthouse, Town Hall and police headquarters in the 19th and 20th centuries. Since 1970, it has been the Tourist Office. The large room upstairs is used for exhibitions.\nThe Bailiwick has been classified as an historic monument since 1886.\n</p>\n<div class=\"mw-heading mw-heading3\"><h3 id=\"Town_hall\">Town hall</h3><span class=\"mw-editsection\"><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">[</span><a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Aire-sur-la-Lys&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7\" title=\"Edit section: Town
