title=\"File:Amberg.ogg\">\u24d8</a></sup></span></span>) is a <a href=\"/wiki/Town#Germany\" title=\"Town\">town</a> in <a href=\"/wiki/Bavaria\" title=\"Bavaria\">Bavaria</a>, <a href=\"/wiki/Germany\" title=\"Germany\">Germany</a>. It is located in the <a href=\"/wiki/Upper_Palatinate\" title=\"Upper Palatinate\">Upper Palatinate</a> about halfway between <a href=\"/wiki/Regensburg\" title=\"Regensburg\">Regensburg</a> and <a href=\"/wiki/Bayreuth\" title=\"Bayreuth\">Bayreuth</a>. \n</p>\n<meta property=\"mw:PageProp/toc\" />\n<div class=\"mw-heading mw-heading2\"><h2 id=\"History\">History</h2><span class=\"mw-editsection\"><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">[</span><a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Amberg&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1\" title=\"Edit section: History\"><span>edit</span></a><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">]</span></span></div>\n<p>The town was first mentioned in 1034 with the name <b>Ammenberg</b>. It became an important trading centre in the <a href=\"/wiki/Middle_Ages\" title=\"Middle Ages\">Middle Ages</a>, exporting mainly <a href=\"/wiki/Iron_ore\" title=\"Iron ore\">iron ore</a> and iron products. In 1269, together with <a href=\"/wiki/Bamberg\" title=\"Bamberg\">Bamberg</a>, the town became subordinate to the <a href=\"/wiki/Wittelsbach\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Wittelsbach\">Wittelsbach</a> dynasty which ruled Bavaria.\n</p><p>In 1329 the town and the entire region fell to the Palatinate branch of the <a href=\"/wiki/Wittelsbach\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Wittelsbach\">Wittelsbach</a> family. The region adopted the name Upper Palatinate. It was no longer part of the duchy of Bavaria politically, though in geographic terms it was regarded as Bavarian and the region was part of the Bavarian circle in the organization of the <a href=\"/wiki/Imperial_Circle\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Imperial Circle\">Imperial Circles</a>. In the 16th century, the rulers of Upper Palatinate turned to <a href=\"/wiki/Protestantism\" title=\"Protestantism\">Protestantism</a>. The town turned to <a href=\"/wiki/Lutheran_church\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Lutheran church\">Lutheranism</a>. Later attempts of the ruling family to introduce the more radical <a href=\"/wiki/Calvinism\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Calvinism\">Calvinism</a> failed due to the reluctance of its citizens. In 1628 Amberg and Upper Palatinate became part of the electorate of Bavaria. The inhabitants were given the choice to return to <a href=\"/wiki/Catholicism\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Catholicism\">Catholicism</a> or emigrate. Many families left the town and moved to the <a href=\"/wiki/Free_Imperial_Cities\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Free Imperial Cities\">Free Imperial Cities</a> of <a href=\"/wiki/Regensburg\" title=\"Regensburg\">Regensburg</a> and <a href=\"/wiki/Nuremberg\" title=\"Nuremberg\">Nuremberg</a>.\n</p><p>On 24&#160;August 1796, during the <a href=\"/wiki/French_Revolutionary_Wars\" title=\"French Revolutionary Wars\">French Revolutionary Wars</a>, a major battle, the <a href=\"/wiki/Battle_of_Amberg\" title=\"Battle of Amberg\">Battle of Amberg</a> was fought in the city and its environs at which 35,000&#160;French, under the command of <a href=\"/wiki/Jean_Baptiste_Jourdan\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Jean Baptiste Jourdan\">Jean Baptiste Jourdan</a> fought with 40,000 Austrians under the command of <a href=\"/wiki/Archduke_Charles,_Duke_of_Teschen\" title=\"Archduke Charles, Duke of Teschen\">Archduke Charles</a>; the French suffered significantly more losses in the Austrian victory. Amberg was the regional capital of <a href=\"/wiki/Upper_Palatinate\" title=\"Upper Palatinate\">Upper Palatinate</a> until 1810 when power was transferred to the larger city of <a href=\"/wiki/Regensburg\" title=\"Regensburg\">Regensburg</a>.\n</p><p>After <a href=\"/wiki/World_War_II\" title=\"World War II\">World War II</a>, when Bavaria fell into the American Sector, Amberg was home to Pond Barracks, a <a href=\"/wiki/United_States_Army\" title=\"United States Army\">United States Army</a> post. <a href=\"/wiki/I.F.Stone\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"I.F.Stone\">I.F.Stone</a> writes about it in his book <a href=\"/wiki/Underground_to_Palestine\" title=\"Underground to Palestine\">Underground to Palestine</a> (pp.&#160;31ff). The post was closed in 1992 and the facility turned over to the local community for housing, most of it for social housing.\n</p><p>In late 2018, the town was the site of the Amberg attacks<sup id=\"cite_ref-3\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-3\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>3<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> resulting in <a href=\"/wiki/Rainer_Wendt\" title=\"Rainer Wendt\">Rainer Wendt</a> asking the federal government to take a stand on the case. The city was reported to be \"in a state of emergency.\"<sup id=\"cite_ref-4\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-4\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>4<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> <a href=\"/wiki/Joachim_Herrmann_(CSU)\" title=\"Joachim Herrmann (CSU)\">Joachim Herrmann</a>, the Bavarian minister of the Interior, visited Amberg for consultations.<sup id=\"cite_ref-5\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-5\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>5<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> <a href=\"/wiki/Horst_Seehofer\" title=\"Horst Seehofer\">Horst Seehofer</a>, the federal minister of the Interior, said \"the violent attacks (were) worrisome.\"<sup id=\"cite_ref-6\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-6\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>6<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup>\n</p>\n<div class=\"mw-heading mw-heading3\"><h3 id=\"Jewish_history\">Jewish history</h3><span class=\"mw-editsection\"><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">[</span><a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Amberg&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2\" title=\"Edit section: Jewish history\"><span>edit</span></a><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">]</span></span></div>\n<p><a href=\"/wiki/Jews\" title=\"Jews\">Jews</a> had settled in Amberg before 1294, when the first documentation can be found. Shortly after, in 1298, thirteen of the town Jews died during the <a href=\"/wiki/Rindfleisch_massacres\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Rindfleisch massacres\">Rindfleisch massacres</a>.<sup id=\"cite_ref-jvl_7-0\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-jvl-7\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>7<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> Nevertheless, in 1347 six families received permission to settle in Amberg and twenty years after, in 1367, a <a href=\"/wiki/Yeshivah\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Yeshivah\">Yeshivah</a> was opened in it, although the Jewish community was expelled from Amberg in 1403.<sup id=\"cite_ref-jvl_7-1\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-jvl-7\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>7<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> Upon the expulsion, the <a href=\"/wiki/Synagogue\" title=\"Synagogue\">synagogue</a> was annexed to the nearby church.<sup id=\"cite_ref-8\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-8\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>8<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> Twelve Jews remained in town in 1942. to <a href=\"/wiki/Germany\" title=\"Germany\">Germany</a>, the <a href=\"/wiki/Jewish\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Jewish\">Jewish</a> population grew to about 275 in 2003.<sup
