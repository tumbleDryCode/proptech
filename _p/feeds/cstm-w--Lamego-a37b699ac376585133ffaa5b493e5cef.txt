Located on the shores of the <a href=\"/wiki/Balsem%C3%A3o_River\" title=\"Balsem\u00e3o River\">Balsem\u00e3o River</a>, the municipality has a population of 26,691,<sup id=\"cite_ref-ine_1-0\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-ine-1\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>1<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> in an area of 165.42&#160;km<sup>2</sup>.<sup id=\"cite_ref-dgt_2-0\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-dgt-2\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>2<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup>\n</p><p>With origins before the Roman occupation of the <a href=\"/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula\" title=\"Iberian Peninsula\">Iberian Peninsula</a>, Lamego is known for its historic city center, having a long history as a principal city of the former <a href=\"/wiki/Tr%C3%A1s-os-Montes_e_Alto_Douro_Province\" title=\"Tr\u00e1s-os-Montes e Alto Douro Province\">Tr\u00e1s-os-Montes e Alto Douro Province</a>. Legend holds that the first <a href=\"/wiki/Portuguese_Cortes\" title=\"Portuguese Cortes\">Portuguese Cortes</a> were held in Lamego, in 1143. The <a href=\"/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Lamego\" title=\"Roman Catholic Diocese of Lamego\">Roman Catholic Diocese of Lamego</a> is based in the city center.\n</p>\n<meta property=\"mw:PageProp/toc\" />\n<div class=\"mw-heading mw-heading2\"><h2 id=\"Etymology\">Etymology</h2><span class=\"mw-editsection\"><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">[</span><a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Lamego&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1\" title=\"Edit section: Etymology\"><span>edit</span></a><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">]</span></span></div>\n<p>The toponymic name <i>Lamego</i> was derived from <i>Lamaecus</i>, a <a href=\"/wiki/Hispano-Celtic\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Hispano-Celtic\">Hispano-Celtic</a> and Roman derivative referring to owners of agrarian titles in the 3rd century around the local castle.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_3-0\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-3\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>3<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id=\"cite_ref-4\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-4\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>4<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup>\n</p>\n<div class=\"mw-heading mw-heading2\"><h2 id=\"History\">History</h2><span class=\"mw-editsection\"><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">[</span><a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Lamego&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2\" title=\"Edit section: History\"><span>edit</span></a><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">]</span></span></div>\n<figure class=\"mw-halign-left\" typeof=\"mw:File/Thumb\"><a href=\"/wiki/File:Lamego_-_Castelo_de_Lamego_(1).jpg\" class=\"mw-file-description\"><img src=\"//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2d/Lamego_-_Castelo_de_Lamego_%281%29.jpg/150px-Lamego_-_Castelo_de_Lamego_%281%29.jpg\" decoding=\"async\" width=\"150\" height=\"225\" class=\"mw-file-element\" srcset=\"//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2d/Lamego_-_Castelo_de_Lamego_%281%29.jpg/225px-Lamego_-_Castelo_de_Lamego_%281%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2d/Lamego_-_Castelo_de_Lamego_%281%29.jpg/300px-Lamego_-_Castelo_de_Lamego_%281%29.jpg 2x\" data-file-width=\"2362\" data-file-height=\"3543\" /></a><figcaption><a href=\"/wiki/Castelo_de_Lamego\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Castelo de Lamego\">Lamego Castle</a>, with origins in the 5th century.</figcaption></figure>\n<p>The area around Lamego was inhabited by <a href=\"/wiki/Ligures\" title=\"Ligures\">Ligures</a> and <a href=\"/wiki/Turduli\" title=\"Turduli\">Turduli</a>, and during the Roman presence it was occupied by <a href=\"/wiki/Coelerni\" title=\"Coelerni\">Coelerni</a>, which left behind several monuments.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_3-1\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-3\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>3<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> Due to the placement of the castle, it is likely that a <a href=\"/wiki/Castro_culture\" title=\"Castro culture\">castro</a> originally existed on the site. During the <i>Inquiri\u00e7\u00f5es</i> (<i>Inventory/Enquiries</i>) of King Afonso (during the 13th century) there was reference to the Castro de Lameco, referred to as a medieval fortification.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_3-2\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-3\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>3<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> Destroyed by the Romans, the inhabitants were forced to descend into the valley and cultivate the land, as part of the Roman reorganization of the land.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_3-3\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-3\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>3<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup>\n</p>\n<div class=\"mw-heading mw-heading3\"><h3 id=\"Middle_Ages\">Middle Ages</h3><span class=\"mw-editsection\"><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">[</span><a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Lamego&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3\" title=\"Edit section: Middle Ages\"><span>edit</span></a><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">]</span></span></div>\n<p>Lamego became Catholic when the <a href=\"/wiki/Visigoth\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Visigoth\">Visigothic</a> king <a href=\"/wiki/Rekared_I\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Rekared I\">Rekared I</a> converted to Catholicism. In 569, during the Council of Lugo, there appeared references to Sardin\u00e1rio the <a href=\"/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Lamego\" title=\"Roman Catholic Diocese of Lamego\">Bishop of Lamego</a>.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_3-4\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-3\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>3<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> During the reign of Sisebuto (612-621), the Visigothic monarch coined currency from Lamego, indicating the importance of the region to commerce and culture.\n</p><p>Just outside the city center is the tiny 7th century <a href=\"/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Pedro_de_Balsem%C3%A3o\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"S\u00e3o Pedro de Balsem\u00e3o\">S\u00e3o Pedro de Balsem\u00e3o</a> Chapel, a <a href=\"/wiki/Visigoths\" title=\"Visigoths\">Visigothic</a> chapel believed to be the oldest in Portugal (and second oldest in Europe).<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_3-5\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-3\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>3<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup>\n</p><p>The region alternated between Christian and Muslim hands during the early <a href=\"/wiki/Reconquista\" title=\"Reconquista\">Reconquista</a> Period. The city was first conquered by <a href=\"/wiki/Alfonso_I_of_Asturias\" title=\"Alfonso I of Asturias\">Alfonso I of Asturias</a> in 741,<sup id=\"cite_ref-5\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-5\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>5<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> and repopulated in 868 by <a href=\"/wiki/Alfonso_III_of_Asturias\" title=\"Alfonso III of Asturias\">Alfonso III</a>.<sup id=\"cite_ref-6\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-6\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>6<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> It fell into Islamic hands briefly again during the late 10th century, until <a href=\"/wiki/Ferdinand_I_of_Le%C3%B3n_and_Castile\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Ferdinand I of Le\u00f3n and Castile\">Ferdinand I of Le\u00f3n and Castile</a> conquered the region definitively on 29 November 1057. As a consequence the bishopric was moved after these events (to later be restored in 1071).<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_3-6\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-3\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>3<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup>\n</p><p>In 1128, the nascent national Egas Moniz, had his tenancy in Lamego while his residence was in <a href=\"/wiki/Britiande\" title=\"Britiande\">Britiande</a>, as master of the Riba\u2013Douro, between <a href=\"/wiki/Vila_Nova_de_Paiva\" title=\"Vila Nova de Paiva\">Paiva</a> and <a href=\"/wiki/T%C3%A1vora_(Santa_Maria_e_S%C3%A3o_Vicente)\" title=\"T\u00e1vora (Santa Maria e S\u00e3o Vicente)\">T\u00e1vora</a> (in addition to the lands of C\u00f4a).<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_3-7\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-3\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>3<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup>\n</p>\n<figure class=\"mw-default-size mw-halign-left\" typeof=\"mw:File/Thumb\"><a href=\"/wiki/File:Lamego,_S%C3%A9-PM_33447.jpg\" class=\"mw-file-description\"><img src=\"//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/59/Lamego%2C_S%C3%A9-PM_33447.jpg/220px-Lamego%2C_S%C3%A9-PM_33447.jpg\" decoding=\"async\" width=\"220\" height=\"147\" class=\"mw-file-element\" srcset=\"//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/59/Lamego%2C_S%C3%A9-PM_33447.jpg/330px-Lamego%2C_S%C3%A9-PM_33447.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/59/Lamego%2C_S%C3%A9-PM_33447.jpg/440px-Lamego%2C_S%C3%A9-PM_33447.jpg 2x\" data-file-width=\"1000\" data-file-height=\"667\" /></a><figcaption>The Cathedral of Our Lady of the Assumption, built in 1129.</figcaption></figure> \n<p>The most significant moment in the town's history was in 1139, when nobles declared <a href=\"/wiki/Afonso_Henriques\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Afonso Henriques\">Afonso Henriques</a> to be Portugal's first king. The town's Gothic cathedral was built by him, although only the <a href=\"/wiki/Romanesque_architecture\" title=\"Romanesque architecture\">Romanesque</a> tower is left from the original building, with its carved Renaissance portal and fine cloister dating from the 16th and 18th centuries. The 12th-century castle preserves a fine keep and a very old and unusual <a href=\"/wiki/Cistern\" title=\"Cistern\">cistern</a> with <a href=\"/wiki/Monogram\" title=\"Monogram\">monograms</a> of master masons.\n</p><p>King <a href=\"/wiki/Sancho_I_of_Portugal\" title=\"Sancho I of Portugal\">Sancho I</a> issued a charter of independence in 1191, as the local community grew around two poles: the ecclesiastical parishes of <a href=\"/w/index.php?title=S%C3%A9_(Lamego)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1\" class=\"new\" title=\"S\u00e9 (Lamego) (page does not exist)\">S\u00e9</a> and <a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Castelo_(Lamego)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1\" class=\"new\" title=\"Castelo (Lamego) (page does not exist)\">Castelo</a>.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_3-8\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-3\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>3<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> In 1290, King <a href=\"/wiki/Denis_of_Portugal\" title=\"Denis of Portugal\">Denis</a> provided a market charter to the city, attracting merchants from <a href=\"/wiki/Crown_of_Castile\" title=\"Crown of Castile\">Castile</a> and <a href=\"/wiki/Emirate_of_Granada\" title=\"Emirate of Granada\">Granada</a> with their oriental spices and textiles.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_3-9\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-3\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>3<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup>\n</p><p>Lamego had a privileged positioned on the routes from western Iberia, as a transit point within the settlements of the Al\u00e9m-Douro, <a href=\"/wiki/Braga\" title=\"Braga\">Braga</a> and <a href=\"/wiki/Guimar%C3%A3es\" title=\"Guimar\u00e3es\">Guimar\u00e3es</a>, from <a href=\"/wiki/Alc%C3%A2ntara,_Lisbon\" title=\"Alc\u00e2ntara, Lisbon\">Alc\u00e2ntara</a> and <a href=\"/wiki/M%C3%A9rida,_Spain\" title=\"M\u00e9rida, Spain\">M\u00e9rida</a> to <a href=\"/wiki/C%C3%B3rdoba,_Spain\" title=\"C\u00f3rdoba, Spain\">C\u00f3rdoba</a> and <a href=\"/wiki/Seville\" title=\"Seville\">Seville</a>.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_3-10\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-3\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>3<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> It was also one of the preferred routes on the <i>Saint James Way</i> pilgrimage to <a href=\"/wiki/Santiago_de_Compostela\" title=\"Santiago de Compostela\">Santiago de Compostela</a>. But, two events changed the economic and social circumstances in the region: the conquest of Granada which drove the last of the Moors from the Peninsula; and the discovery of the maritime connection to India, which resulted in a slow decline for the region.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_3-11\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-3\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>3<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup>\n</p>\n<div class=\"mw-heading mw-heading3\"><h3 id=\"Early_modern_era\">Early modern era</h3><span class=\"mw-editsection\"><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">[</span><a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Lamego&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4\" title=\"Edit section: Early modern era\"><span>edit</span></a><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">]</span></span></div>\n<figure class=\"mw-halign-right\" typeof=\"mw:File/Thumb\"><a href=\"/wiki/File:Lamego_remedios.jpg\" class=\"mw-file-description\"><img src=\"//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a3/Lamego_remedios.jpg/190px-Lamego_remedios.jpg\" decoding=\"async\" width=\"190\" height=\"285\" class=\"mw-file-element\" srcset=\"//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a3/Lamego_remedios.jpg/285px-Lamego_remedios.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a3/Lamego_remedios.jpg/380px-Lamego_remedios.jpg 2x\" data-file-width=\"400\" data-file-height=\"600\" /></a><figcaption>Sanctuary of Our Lady of Rem\u00e9dios, built in 1750.</figcaption></figure>\n<p><a href=\"/wiki/Manuel_I_of_Portugal\" title=\"Manuel I of Portugal\">Manuel I of Portugal</a> issued a <a href=\"/wiki/Foral\" title=\"Foral\">foral</a> (<i>charter</i>) in 1514. Also in the 16th century, Manuel de Noronha (one of the more notable prelates of Lamego) was appointed the Bishop of Lamego, occupying the role for the next 18 years and referred to the <i>\"great builder\"</i>, responsible for the cult of <i>Nossa Senhora dos Rem\u00e9dios</i> (<i>Our Lady of Remedies</i>).<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_3-12\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-3\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>3<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup>\n</p><p>In the 17th and 18th century, the <i>solares</i> (<i>country estates</i>) were slowly constructed in Lamego, resulting from the wine commerce down the Douro.\n</p><p>The economic growth of <a href=\"/wiki/Viticulture\" title=\"Viticulture\">viticulture</a> along the Douro resulted in the issuance of a writ by <a href=\"/wiki/Sebasti%C3%A3o_Jos%C3%A9_de_Carvalho_e_Melo,_1st_Marquis_of_Pombal\" title=\"Sebasti\u00e3o Jos\u00e9 de Carvalho e Melo, 1st Marquis of Pombal\">Sebasti\u00e3o Jos\u00e9 de Carvalho e Melo, 1st Marquis of Pombal</a>, to designate the area as the <i>Regi\u00e3o Demarcada do Douro</i> and the establishment of the <i>Companhia Geral da Agricultura das Vinhas do Alto Douro</i> (<i>General Company of the Wine Agriculture of the Upper Douro</i>).<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_3-13\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-3\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>3<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup>\n</p><p>In 1835, Lamego was the capital of the district, but lost this title to Viseu (December 1835), under the authority of the King's Minister <a href=\"/wiki/Lu%C3%ADs_Mouzinho_de_Albuquerque\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Lu\u00eds Mouzinho de Albuquerque\">Lu\u00eds Mouzinho de Albuquerque</a>. \nIn 1919, in an attempt to restore the monarchy, Lamego became the capital of the district for 24 days.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_3-14\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-3\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>3<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup>\n</p><p>During the second half of the 19th century, during the presidency of the Viscount of Guedes Teixeira, Lamego begins a process of modernization with the construction of new avenues.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_3-15\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-3\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>3<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup>\n</p><p>After the establishment of the <a href=\"/wiki/Portuguese_First_Republic\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Portuguese First Republic\">First Republic</a>, during the presidency of Alfredo de Sousa, Lamego undergoes a new phase of building, including the construction a bridge over the Coura River.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_3-16\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-3\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>3<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup>\n</p>\n<div class=\"mw-heading mw-heading2\"><h2 id=\"Twin_towns_\u2013_sister_cities\"><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">]</span></span></div>\n<div class=\"mw-heading mw-heading3\"><h3 id=\"Climate\">Climate</h3><span class=\"mw-editsection\"><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">[</span><a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Lamego&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7\" title=\"Edit section: Climate\"><span>edit</span></a><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">]</span></span></div>\n<p>Lamego has a <a href=\"/wiki/Mediterranean_climate\" title=\"Mediterranean climate\">Mediterranean climate</a> with some continental influence; with warm, dry summers and cool, wet winters. The nearest weather station is located in Bigorne, almost 400&#160;m (1,300&#160;ft) higher in altitude, and having cooler temperatures as such. Rem\u00e9dios Park.</figcaption></figure>\n<p>The principal activities in this region are tied to the service sector, with commerce and agriculture also representing an important part of the economy. In particular <a href=\"/wiki/Viticulture\" title=\"Viticulture\">viticulture</a>, since the municipality (and other municipalities in the region) have been designated as part of the <i>Denomina\u00e7\u00e3o de Origem Controlada</i> (DOC), a control used to classify wine products as a locally produced product of origin, for national and international marketing.\n</p><p>Lamego has a large shopping centre, with approximately 30 main shops, three malls of medium-size and several small traditional shops. Industrial activities are concentrated in the industrial zone in the parish of V\u00e1rzea de Abrunhais. Lamego textiles are also promoted but tend to be small-scale production runs, with few medium- to large-companies involved in mass-production.\n</p><p>The tourist sector is primarily associated with the monuments and religious buildings, and has been a growing part of the local economy.
