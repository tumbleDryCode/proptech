class=\"infobox-data\">\u039a\u039c</td></tr></tbody></table>\n<p><b>Messini</b> (<a href=\"/wiki/Greek_language\" title=\"Greek language\">Greek</a>: <span lang=\"el\">\u039c\u03b5\u03c3\u03c3\u03ae\u03bd\u03b7</span>, before 1867: \u039d\u03b7\u03c3\u03af <i>Nisi</i>)<sup id=\"cite_ref-eetaa_2-0\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-eetaa-2\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>2<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> is a municipal unit (<i>dimotiki enotita</i>) and the seat town (<i>edra</i>) of the <a href=\"/wiki/Communities_and_Municipalities_of_Greece\" class=\"mw-redirect\" title=\"Communities and Municipalities of Greece\">municipality</a> (<i>dimos</i>) of Messini within the regional unit (<i>perifereiaki enotita</i>) of <a href=\"/wiki/Messenia\" title=\"Messenia\">Messenia</a> in the region (<i>perifereia</i>) of <a href=\"/wiki/Peloponnese_(region)\" title=\"Peloponnese (region)\">Peloponnese</a>, one of 13 regions into which <a href=\"/wiki/Greece\" title=\"Greece\">Greece</a> has been divided.<sup id=\"cite_ref-Kallikratis_3-0\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-Kallikratis-3\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>3<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> Before 2011 the same hierarchy prevailed, according to Law 2539 of 1997, the Kapodistrias Plan, except that Messenia was a <i>nomos</i> and the Municipal unit was a locality (<i>topiko diamerisma</i>). The <i>dimos</i> existed under both laws, but not with the same constituents.\n</p><p>Messini (modern pronunciation) is not to be confused with its ancient namesake, <a href=\"/wiki/Messene\" title=\"Messene\">Messene</a>, located 15 kilometres (9.3 miles) to the north in the shadow of Mount <a href=\"/wiki/Ithome\" title=\"Ithome\">Ithome</a>. Ancient Messene is an equally large but abandoned site of ruins partially occupied by the small village of Mavrommati.\n</p>\n<meta property=\"mw:PageProp/toc\" />\n<div class=\"mw-heading mw-heading2\"><h2 id=\"Geography\">Geography</h2><span class=\"mw-editsection\"><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">[</span><a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Messini&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1\" title=\"Edit section: Geography\"><span>edit</span></a><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">]</span></span></div>\n<p>The modern town has some 6,000 inhabitants. It is the second largest municipality of Messinia regional unit. The town centre is 3&#160;km (1.9&#160;mi) from the <a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Messinian_Gulf&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1\" class=\"new\" title=\"Messinian Gulf (page does not exist)\">Messinian Gulf</a> coast, near the right bank of the river <a href=\"/wiki/Pamisos_(river)\" title=\"Pamisos (river)\">Pamisos</a>. The major city of Messinia, <a href=\"/wiki/Kalamata\" title=\"Kalamata\">Kalamata</a>, is 10&#160;km (6.2&#160;mi) to the east of Messini and has almost 9 times its population. The town is accessed by <a href=\"/wiki/Greek_National_Road_82\" title=\"Greek National Road 82\">Greek National Road 82</a> (Pylos - Kalamata - Sparta). The <a href=\"/wiki/Kalamata_International_Airport\" title=\"Kalamata International Airport\">Kalamata International Airport</a> is two kilometers to the east. Since 1892, Messini had a railway station on a branch line from the <a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Corinth%E2%80%93Kalamata_railway&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1\" class=\"new\" title=\"Corinth\u2013Kalamata railway (page does not exist)\">Corinth\u2013Kalamata railway</a>. class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">[</span><a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Messini&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3\" title=\"Edit section: Province\"><span>edit</span></a><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">]</span></span></div>\n<p>The province of Messini (<a href=\"/wiki/Greek_language\" title=\"Greek language\">Greek</a>: <span lang=\"el\">\u0395\u03c0\u03b1\u03c1\u03c7\u03af\u03b1 \u039c\u03b5\u03c3\u03c3\u03ae\u03bd\u03b7\u03c2</span>) was one of the <a href=\"/wiki/Provinces_of_Greece\" title=\"Provinces of Greece\">provinces</a> of the Messenia Prefecture. Its territory corresponded with that of the current municipality Messini (except the municipal units <a href=\"/wiki/Aipeia\" title=\"Aipeia\">Aipeia</a>, <a href=\"/wiki/Petalidi\" title=\"Petalidi\">Petalidi</a> and <a href=\"/wiki/Voufrades\" title=\"Voufrades\">Voufrades</a>) and the municipal units <a href=\"/wiki/Andania\" title=\"Andania\">Andania</a>, <a href=\"/wiki/Meligalas\" title=\"Meligalas\">Meligalas</a> and <a href=\"/wiki/Oichalia,_Messenia\" title=\"Oichalia, Messenia\">Oichalia</a>.<sup id=\"cite_ref-census91_6-0\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-census91-6\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>6<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> It was abolished in 2006.\n</p>\n<div class=\"mw-heading mw-heading2\"><h2 id=\"History\">History</h2><span class=\"mw-editsection\"><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">[</span><a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Messini&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4\" title=\"Edit section: History\"><span>edit</span></a><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">]</span></span></div>\n<p>Messini began its communal existence as Nisi, a settlement placed on an island (n\u0113sos, from which Nisi) in the extensive marshland at the mouth of the Pamisos River.<sup id=\"cite_ref-7\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-7\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>7<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> It was placed in Frankish territory, the Principality of Achaia, dated 1205-1432, consisting of 40 villages in Messenia and Elis. The ethnic content of the population in the vicinity at that time was Greek.  After 1432, when the Byzantine Empire fell and was commandeered by the Ottoman Empire, some Turks entered the region. Except for the names, these distinctions have more or less disappeared in modern Greece.\n</p><p>The government of Greece changed the official name to Messini in 1867.<sup id=\"cite_ref-eetaa_2-1\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-eetaa-2\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#91;</span>2<span class=\"cite-bracket\">&#93;</span></a></sup> <a href=\"/wiki/Baedeker\" title=\"Baedeker\">Baedeker</a>'s for the later 19th century and early 20th centuries referred to Nisi as \"now officially Messini,\" a phrase that was widely used in travelogues of the period. The earliest Baedeker's to do so is the German edition of 1888. Prior to 1887 Nisi is not treated as Messini.  Subsequent references to the place often refer to Nisi as \"the popular name\".\n</p>\n<div class=\"mw-heading mw-heading2\"><h2 id=\"Recent_population_growth\">Recent population growth</h2><span
