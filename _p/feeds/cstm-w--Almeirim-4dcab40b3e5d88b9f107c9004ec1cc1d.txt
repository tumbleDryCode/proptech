class=\"cite-bracket\">]</span></a></sup>\n</p>\n<meta property=\"mw:PageProp/toc\">\n<div class=\"mw-heading mw-heading2\"><h2 id=\"History\">History</h2><span class=\"mw-editsection\"><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">[</span><a href=\"/w/index.php?title=Almeirim&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1\" title=\"Edit section: History\"><span>edit</span></a><span class=\"mw-editsection-bracket\">]</span></span></div>\n<p>There are vestiges of mid-Paleolithic to Mesolithic period along the territory, including traces from Neolithic, Calcolithic and Bronze Age remnants.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_4-0\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-4\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">[</span>4<span class=\"cite-bracket\">]</span></a></sup> The Iron Age also marked this region, with archaeological excavations unearthing settlements and artefacts.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_4-1\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-4\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">[</span>4<span class=\"cite-bracket\">]</span></a></sup>\n</p><p>Roman legions of D\u00e9cimo Junius Brutus<sup id=\"cite_ref-5\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-5\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">[</span>5<span class=\"cite-bracket\">]</span></a></sup> occupied the territory, following the Tagus upstream and disembarking in Santar\u00e9m, where they left their marks.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_4-2\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-4\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">[</span>4<span class=\"cite-bracket\">]</span></a></sup> These, along with other groups, occupied arable lands, beginning in the first century A.D. This included the development of agriculture (particularly wheat and olive orchards) and raising of cattle, supporting the establishment of Almeirim along the margins of the Tagus.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_4-3\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-4\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">[</span>4<span class=\"cite-bracket\">]</span></a></sup>\n</p><p>References to this municipality, began appearing in the 14th century. With rich lands, the territory supported hunting, extending to Santar\u00e9m, and proximities of the Tagus and Lisbon.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_4-4\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-4\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">[</span>4<span class=\"cite-bracket\">]</span></a></sup> This easy access, made Almeirim a preferred place for the Kings of the second Dynasty, and a winter place frequented by members of Corte, becoming known as the <i>Sintra of Winter</i>.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_4-5\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-4\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">[</span>4<span class=\"cite-bracket\">]</span></a></sup> Almeirim, therefore, became an ideal place for relaxation, palace intrigue and romantic encounters in the royal gardens, mixed with resolutions of important negotiations.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_4-6\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-4\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">[</span>4<span class=\"cite-bracket\">]</span></a></sup>\n</p><p>King <a href=\"/wiki/John_I_of_Portugal\" title=\"John I of Portugal\">John I</a>, between 1411 and 1423, ordered the construction of a (castellated) <a href=\"/wiki/Royal_Palace_of_Almeirim\" title=\"Royal Palace of Almeirim\">royal palace</a> and first residences that contributed to the establishment of the town, with the work to the <i>Al-meirim</i>, that included landscaping, drainage and earthen-works.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_4-7\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-4\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">[</span>4<span class=\"cite-bracket\">]</span></a></sup><sup id=\"cite_ref-SIPA_6-0\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-SIPA-6\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">[</span>6<span class=\"cite-bracket\">]</span></a></sup>\n</p><p>In 1483, Almeirim was elevated to the status of municipality, wherein he bestowed a concession on its residents.<sup id=\"cite_ref-SIPA_6-1\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-SIPA-6\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">[</span>6<span class=\"cite-bracket\">]</span></a></sup> As a consequence, in 1490, its first <i>almoxarife</i> (<i>sheriff</i>) \u00c1lvaro Pires Borges was assigned by King D. <a href=\"/wiki/John_II_of_Portugal\" title=\"John II of Portugal\">John II</a> from the royal guard.<sup id=\"cite_ref-SIPA_6-2\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-SIPA-6\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">[</span>6<span class=\"cite-bracket\">]</span></a></sup>\n</p><p>In 1500, the Church of S\u00e3o Jo\u00e3o Baptista was constructed.<sup id=\"cite_ref-SIPA_6-3\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-SIPA-6\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">[</span>6<span class=\"cite-bracket\">]</span></a></sup>\n</p><p>The palace was expanded and improved by <a href=\"/wiki/Manuel_I_of_Portugal\" title=\"Manuel I of Portugal\">Manuel I of Portugal</a> who was in Almeirim several times: in 1510, part of 1513, Christmas of 1514 and all the period between October 1515 and May 1516.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_4-8\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-4\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">[</span>4<span class=\"cite-bracket\">]</span></a></sup> <a href=\"/wiki/John_III_of_Portugal\" title=\"John III of Portugal\">John III of Portugal</a> followed his example, and spent similar time in Almeirim.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_4-9\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-4\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">[</span>4<span class=\"cite-bracket\">]</span></a></sup> He ordered the construction of a royal palace near the <i>Ribeira de Muge</i> (<i>Muja</i> or <i>Mugem</i>), that became known as <i>Pa\u00e7o da Ribeira de Muge</i> and later, the <i>Pa\u00e7o dos Negros', then later the Convent of Nossa Senhora da Serra.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_4-10\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-4\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">[</span>4<span class=\"cite-bracket\">]</span></a></sup></i>\n</p><p>King Sebastian, who frequently visited Almeirim, created a dynastic succession crisis with his disappearance at the Battle of Alc\u00e1cer-Quibir.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_4-11\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-4\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">[</span>4<span class=\"cite-bracket\">]</span></a></sup> Without a successor, the Cortes of Almeirim was opened by Cardinal Henry on 11 January 1580, to decide on the problem of succession.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_4-12\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-4\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">[</span>4<span class=\"cite-bracket\">]</span></a></sup> During the Cortes, Febo Moniz, as magistrate of Lisbon, directed his response to the Cardinal, stating <i>\"Give this your Highness to a Portuguese prince and all will kiss his hand\"</i>.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_4-13\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-4\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">[</span>4<span class=\"cite-bracket\">]</span></a></sup> But, little was resolved, and the Kingdom eventually began to be governed by Philip II of Spain, beginning the reign of Phillipian Dynastic Union, until 1 December 1640.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_4-14\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-4\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">[</span>4<span class=\"cite-bracket\">]</span></a></sup>\n</p><p>At the time Almeirim was visited as a winter resort, where many passed through the roads of the burgh and stayed at the Royal Palace.<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_4-15\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-4\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">[</span>4<span class=\"cite-bracket\">]</span></a></sup> Gil Vicente, the father of Portuguese theatre, presented many of his farses, comedies and plays, for example \"Auto da F\u00e9\" in 1510;  \"Barca da Gl\u00f3ria\" in 1519; tragic-comedy \"Dom Dardos\" at the marriage of Infanta D. Isabel with Charles V, in 1525; and in 1526 he presented the farse \"O Juiz da Beira\", the tragic-comedy \"Templo de Apolo\", \"Breve Sum\u00e1rio da Hist\u00f3ria de Deus\" and \"Di\u00e1logo sobre a Ressurrei\u00e7\u00e3o\".<sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_4-16\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-4\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">[</span>4<span class=\"cite-bracket\">]</span></a></sup> It was also in the palace the Garcia de Resende began printing his <i>Cancioneiro Geral.</i><sup id=\"cite_ref-CMHistoria_4-17\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-CMHistoria-4\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">[</span>4<span class=\"cite-bracket\">]</span></a></sup>\n</p><p>In the <i><a href=\"/wiki/Parochial_Memories_of_1758\" title=\"Parochial Memories of 1758\">Mem\u00f3rias Paroquiais</a></i> (1758) the resident population was just over 302 neighbours.<sup id=\"cite_ref-SIPA_6-4\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-SIPA-6\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">[</span>6<span class=\"cite-bracket\">]</span></a></sup>\n</p><p>With the inauguration of the D. Lu\u00eds bridge in 1881, the connection between Almeirim and Santar\u00e9m, allowed a significant change in the economy.<sup id=\"cite_ref-SIPA_6-5\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-SIPA-6\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">[</span>6<span class=\"cite-bracket\">]</span></a></sup>\n</p><p>In 1935, the <i>Casa do Povo</i>, health centre and dispensary were inaugurated. By the middle of the century (1953), the bullring, a social centre of annual activities was also open to the public.<sup id=\"cite_ref-SIPA_6-6\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-SIPA-6\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">[</span>6<span class=\"cite-bracket\">]</span></a></sup>\n</p><p>On 20 June 1991, the settlement was elevated to the status of city.<sup id=\"cite_ref-SIPA_6-7\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-SIPA-6\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">[</span>6<span class=\"cite-bracket\">]</span></a></sup>\n</p><p>The municipal assembly approved ARU02, designating the <i>\u00c1rea de Reabilita\u00e7\u00e3o Urbana</i> (<i>Urban Rehabilitation Area</i>) for Almeirim (8902/2014, Di\u00e1rio da Rep\u00fablica, 147, S\u00e9rie 1) in order to modernize and recuperate many of the historic buildings in the town.<sup id=\"cite_ref-SIPA_6-8\" class=\"reference\"><a href=\"#cite_note-SIPA-6\"><span class=\"cite-bracket\">[</span>6<span data-file-height=\"2214\"></a><figcaption>A Bowl of Stone Soup</figcaption></figure>\n<p>The city is also known for its gastronomy, to which <a href=\"/wiki/Stone_Soup\" title=\"Stone Soup\">Stone Soup</a> is just one great example. According to the people of Almeirim, a poor friar who was on a pilgrimage stopped in the village of Almeirim and knocked on the door of a house. He was too proud to beg for a bite to eat, so instead, he requested a large pot in which he could make \u201ca delicious and filling.......stone soup\u201d. With arched eyebrows and curious glances, the family invited him into their home and set up a large pot over flickering flames and filled with water. Slowly walking up to the iron clad cauldron, the friar reached into his deep pocket to produce a smooth and well-cleaned stone that he promptly dropped into the boiling water. A little while later he tasted the soup and said that it needed a touch of seasoning.\n</p><p>So, the wife brought him some salt to add, to which he suggested that maybe a little bit of chouri\u00e7o (sausage), or pork belly, would be better. Graciously, she obliged and dropped several thick slices into the pot. Then, the friar asked if she might not have a little something to enrich the soup, such as potatoes or beans from a previous meal. With a broad smile, she agreed, and added a healthy portion into the bubbling water. This banter continued back and forth between the family and the friar before he finally announced that he had indeed made a very delicious and filling soup. When the soup was done, the friar fished the stone out of the pot, washed and dried it off, and plopped it back in his pocket for the next time.\n</p><p>Nowadays Almeirim is a gastronomical destination for Portuguese people, as well as for foreign people, willing to taste this soup.\n</p>\n<div class=\"mw-heading mw-heading2\"><h2 id=\"Notable_citizens\">Notable citizens</h2><span
